# Study the transformation of a galvanizing line to expand its product range

## About the Project

As part of an industrial line Master Plan, we studied the possibility of expanding the range of manufactured products to access new markets with higher value added.

One of the opportunities concerned the introduction of a metallic coating, requiring manufacturing conditions different from those of existing products.

**The challenge consisted in transforming an installation in operations to accommodate a new family of products, while preserving its ability to manufacture its historical range.**

This evolution involved significant modifications to certain equipment, particularly around the metal bath, as well as an analysis of their consequences on the entire process.

The issue therefore went beyond technical feasibility: it was necessary to determine whether diversification could be carried out under economically and industrially acceptable conditions.

## Objectives

I contributed to a feasibility study aimed at:

- Understanding the technical requirements of the new coating.
- Identifying necessary modifications on the existing line.
- Preserving the possibility of manufacturing historical products.
- Comparing different equipment architectures.
- Evaluating necessary investments.
- Estimating works duration and associated production stoppages.
- Measuring operations constraints and risks.
- Building a technical and budgetary proposal enabling investment arbitration.

## Approach

### 1. Analyze the industrial opportunity

The project was part of a strategic reflection on the evolution of the line towards technical products with higher value added.

The first step consisted in understanding the characteristics of the new coating and the consequences of its introduction on an installation designed for another product range.

It was necessary to study, among other things, the compatibility of existing equipment, constraints linked to the change of metal bath, and conditions allowing to maintain multi-product operations.

### 2. Conduct a technical benchmark on existing installations

I participated in a benchmark with a process expert on two industrial installations already producing comparable coatings.

We notably studied:

- Metal bath changeover and transfer operations.
- Pumping systems and temporary storage of molten metal.
- Equipment used to manage different compositions.
- Safety constraints.
- Cleanliness and maintenance requirements.
- Compatibility of materials and equipment with the new coating.

**This benchmark was a decisive step in identifying viable solutions, their constraints, and the key points to integrate into the studies.**

### 3. Compare multiple transformation scenarios

Drawing on lessons from the benchmark, I contributed to preliminary studies comparing different technical architectures.

Two major solutions were notably examined.

**Scenario 1 — Independent ladles and liquid metal transfer**

This configuration relied on installations enabling separate management of baths and organization of transfer operations.

It required studying pumping equipment, storage capacities, connections, and safety conditions.

**Scenario 2 — Multiple crucibles with movement**

This approach relied on using multiple crucibles and means for their handling or replacement.

It involved significant mechanical adaptations and civil engineering works.

The comparison did not focus solely on nominal operation: transition operations between products, maintenance interventions, and restart conditions also had to be taken into account.

### 4. Evaluate modifications with the New Works teams

I worked with technical teams and New Works to identify necessary adaptations for the line.

The study integrated potential consequences on several process functions:

- Management and change of the metal bath.
- Crucible and associated equipment.
- Civil engineering and layout.
- Squeezing systems.
- Strip cooling.
- Peripheral equipment and interfaces.
- Operating, safety, and maintenance conditions.

**The goal was to build a global vision of the transformations, rather than limiting the study to the main equipment.**

This approach also allowed identifying dependencies between works and risks related to integrating new equipment into an existing installation.

### 5. Build construction scenarios and measure impact on production

A decisive dimension of the project concerned the duration of the works.

Transforming a line in operation required taking into account:

- Preparation of interventions.
- Dismantling and installation operations.
- Civil engineering works.
- Equipment adaptations.
- Testing and restart operations.
- Line downtime duration.

The analysis also had to integrate the continuity of historical production and the consequences of stoppages on industrial activity.

**Line immobilization duration gradually emerged as a major feasibility criterion.**

A technically feasible solution could indeed become difficult to accept if its implementation required too long a shutdown of the installation.

### 6. Establish an investment proposal

The studies enabled structuring the envisaged modifications, technical scenarios, and main implementation constraints.

I contributed to preparing pre-study files and consolidating the elements necessary for arbitration.

The final proposal included:

- The envisaged technical solutions.
- Modifications to equipment and infrastructure.
- Work requirements.
- Schedule estimates.
- Consequences on industrial availability.
- Associated risks.
- An investment budget envelope.

This approach enabled transforming a product development opportunity into a concrete decision-support file.

## My role

I contributed to the feasibility study and preparation of line evolution scenarios, leveraging my process expertise and knowledge of operations constraints.

This project required me to integrate process, equipment, works, scheduling, and investment dimensions within an industrial decision-making approach.

## Results

**A finalized feasibility study, enabling an informed decision on line evolution.**

**The project was ultimately not implemented on the studied line, primarily due to the excessive impact of works on production downtime duration.**

This decision illustrates the value of the study: it highlighted a decisive industrial constraint before committing investments.

## Lessons learned

>Technical feasibility alone is insufficient to justify an industrial investment: production tool availability can sometimes be the deciding factor.
> 

**This project illustrates my ability to lead a multidisciplinary industrial study, compare investment scenarios, and inform a strategic decision by integrating the technical, economic, and operational realities of a production line.**
