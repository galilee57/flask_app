# Lead/coordinate maintenance and reliability for a fleet of 2,000 industrial equipment

## Context

The performance of an industrial site relies on the availability and reliability of its production equipment.

As a **Mechanical Maintenance Engineer**, I managed a fleet of **2,000 industrial equipment**, including rotating machinery, pumps, gearboxes, conveyors, and hydraulic and pneumatic systems.

This responsibility required balancing several requirements: ensuring production continuity, controlling maintenance costs, organizing interventions, managing technical risks, and ensuring team safety.

**The objective: maintain installations under optimal conditions of safety, availability, and performance, while controlling human, technical, and financial resources.**

## Approach

I led mechanical maintenance at the site around six complementary axes.

### 1. Define and lead the maintenance strategy

Organization and monitoring of different maintenance policies:

- **Preventive**: definition and optimization of maintenance and inspection plans.
- **Condition-based and predictive**: exploitation of vibration analysis, thermography, and equipment monitoring data.
- **Corrective**: failure diagnosis, prioritization of interventions, and organization of repairs.

The objective was to adapt maintenance strategies to the criticality of equipment and their actual operating conditions.

### 2. Control budgets and investments

Oversight of financial resources dedicated to mechanical maintenance:

- Preparation and monitoring of the annual budget.
- Management of operating expenses (OPEX).
- Preparation and monitoring of investments (CAPEX).
- Arbitration between repair, replacement, and reliability enhancement.
- Negotiation and monitoring of supplier services.

This approach allowed integrating economic issues into technical decisions.

### 3. Secure spare parts

Definition and monitoring of the policy for managing critical spare parts:

- Identification of strategic components.
- Analysis of stockout risks.
- Definition of stock levels and replenishment.
- Coordination with suppliers and the warehouse.
- Search for a balance between parts availability and storage costs.

### 4. Lead and coordinate teams and subcontractors

Organization and coordination of maintenance activities:

- Scheduling interventions.
- Allocating priorities based on production constraints.
- Leading and supporting technicians.
- Coordinating external companies.
- Monitoring work progress.
- Developing the technical skills of teams.

Operational management was an essential lever to ensure the quality and efficiency of interventions.

### 5. Diagnose and improve equipment reliability

Intervention on complex mechanical issues:

- Diagnosis of failures in rotating machinery.
- Analysis of problems on mechanical transmissions, pumps, and reducers.
- Investigations into hydraulic and pneumatic systems.
- Root cause analysis (RCA, 5 Whys).
- Use of FMEA to identify and prioritize risks.
- Definition and follow-up of reliability improvement actions.

The approach aimed to go beyond immediate repairs to address the mechanisms responsible for recurring failures.

### 6. Ensure intervention safety

Integration of HSE requirements into all maintenance activities:

- Preparation of interventions and risk analysis.
- Application of lockout/tagout and safety procedures.
- Organizing work in coactivity.
- Leading safety briefings.
- Monitoring regulatory requirements and periodic inspections.
- Coordinating interventions with specific risks.

## My Role

As a **Mechanical Maintenance Engineer for the site**, I held a cross-functional responsibility combining technical expertise, resource management, and operational management.

My missions included:

- Defining and piloting maintenance strategies for the asset portfolio.
- Managing budgets and mechanical investments.
- Coordinating maintenance teams and subcontractors.
- Managing critical spare parts.
- Expertise and resolution of complex failures.
- Leading reliability improvement actions.
- Controlling risks and ensuring intervention safety.

This role led me to make technical and economic decisions in an industrial environment where equipment availability directly conditions production performance.

## Results

- **Management of a portfolio of 2,000 industrial assets.**
- Piloting preventive, condition-based, and corrective maintenance activities.
- Managing maintenance budgets and mechanical investments.
- Organizing and coordinating teams and subcontracting companies.
- Structuring the management of critical spare parts.
- Conducting failure analyses and reliability improvement actions.
- Integrating safety into the preparation and execution of interventions.

## Lessons Learned

**Industrial maintenance performance relies on a balance between technical expertise, failure anticipation, economic control, and team management.**

Three principles guided my approach:

- **Prioritize based on criticality**: Maintenance resources must be concentrated on assets presenting the highest risks to safety and production.
- **Think in terms of total cost**: Decisions regarding repair, replacement, storage, or investment must integrate the consequences of unavailability.
- **Combine technical reliability with collective efficiency**: Diagnostic tools and maintenance methods only produce sustainable results when integrated into team practices.

**This experience forms the foundation of my expertise in industrial maintenance and reliability, combining management, budget control, mechanical diagnosis, and continuous improvement.**
