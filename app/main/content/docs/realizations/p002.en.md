# Driving improvements to industrial furnace refractories to reduce defects and operating costs

## Context

In my role as a process engineer, I was responsible for the operational performance and spending of a stand using thermal equipment.

Refractories were a critical issue for the operation of the installations.

Exposed to severe operating conditions, they required monthly replacement operations.

Their lifespan depended on several factors: material quality, installation conditions, gunning operations, and operating practices.

Premature degradation could lead to consequences across several dimensions:

- **Product quality**, particularly due to refractory particle entrainment.
- **Equipment integrity**, with a risk of casing degradation.
- **Operating costs**, linked to refractory consumption and replacement interventions.
- **Supply chain management**, due to the volumes consumed and necessary stock levels.

I launched a continuous improvement project to better understand wear mechanisms, reduce losses, and strengthen control over these operations.

## Objectives

- Improve refractory lifespan.
- Reduce quality defects related to refractory particle entrainment.
- Limit equipment degradation risks.
- Reduce material volumes replaced during furnace reloading.
- Optimize consumption and spending.
- Reduce stock levels and replenishment needs.
- Improve installation, gunning, and operating practices.
- Implement a sustainable performance monitoring approach.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Build a diagnosis based on losses

I structured the approach around a **loss matrix** to identify the main factors contributing to refractory consumption and observed malfunctions.

This method allowed us to organize problems, prioritize issues, and direct investigations toward the most significant causes.

The goal was to go beyond simply noting high consumption and understand the mechanisms responsible for wear and losses.

### 2. Observe practices on site

I complemented the analysis with direct observations of operations and operating conditions.

This step was essential to understand the interactions between:

- Characteristics of refractory materials.
- Preparation and installation conditions.
- Gunning practices.
- Thermal and mechanical stresses during operations.
- Wear and degradation phenomena.

Exchanges with operational teams allowed us to confront hypotheses with field realities.

### 3. Identify and prioritize causes

Based on the loss matrix and observations, I organized the search for degradation causes.

The work aimed to distinguish factors related to the material, its implementation, and usage conditions.

This analysis enabled us to define improvement priorities and focus efforts on actions with the best operational potential.

### 4. Develop and implement an action plan

I led the development and implementation of an action plan aimed at improving refractory performance.

The approach involved teams concerned with maintenance operations, material implementation, and furnace operations.

My role consisted of organizing actions, monitoring their execution, and verifying their effects on expected results.

### 5. Monitor lifespan and consumption

One of the key indicators was the **volume of refractories replaced at each furnace reloading**.

This indicator allowed us to track wear evolution and assess the effects of improvements.

Monitoring also covered quality defects related to refractory handling and material consumption.

This approach directly linked technical actions to their consequences on industrial performance.

### 6. Optimize stocks and supply

Improving the lifespan of refractories also enabled us to address material requirements.

Reduced consumption volumes contributed to lowering storage and replenishment needs.

The project thus extended beyond the purely technical scope to include an economic and logistical dimension.

### 7. Extend the approach to other gunning operations

I also led other improvement projects covering gunning operations, notably the application of refractory materials to distributors.

These approaches were based on the same principles: observe practices, understand degradation mechanisms, identify losses, and implement corrective actions.

They enabled us to reuse a continuous improvement method across different industrial equipment.

## My role

**As a process engineer, I led this continuous improvement project with direct responsibility for industrial performance and associated costs.**

I managed the project by mobilizing the skills of operational and technical teams.

## Technologies and methods

**Technical areas:** industrial furnaces, refractory materials, gunning, maintenance, thermal operations, product quality.

**Methods:** continuous improvement, loss matrix, field observations, root cause analysis, prioritization, action plan, indicator tracking.

**Indicators:** defects related to refractories, volumes replaced during each reload, consumption, inventory levels, replenishments.

**Skills:** industrial management, performance steering, cost control, operational coordination, problem-solving.

## Results

The project enabled several improvements:

- **Reduction in quality defects** related to the entrainment of refractory particles.
- **Improved refractory lifespan**, evidenced by the reduction in volumes replaced during furnace reloads.
- **Reduction in refractory material inventory.**
- **Decreased replenishment requirements.**
- Implementation of a structured approach for analyzing and improving practices.

## Lessons Learned

### 1. A loss matrix helps focus efforts

Structuring losses and prioritizing root causes prevents scattering actions and allows addressing the most significant issues.

### 2. A maintenance issue can impact the entire value chain

Refractory wear can potentially affect product quality, equipment reliability, costs, and supply chains.

Improvement must therefore be approached in a cross-functional manner.

### 3. Continuous improvement must link technical actions to economic outcomes

Reducing consumption also allows action on expenses, inventory levels, and supply flows.

**This project illustrates my ability to lead a complete continuous improvement approach: starting from observed losses, building a technical diagnosis, mobilizing teams, implementing actions, and measuring their effects on quality, equipment lifespan, and operational costs.**
