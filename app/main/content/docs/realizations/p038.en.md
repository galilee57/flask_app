# Structure programming and analysis of production campaigns

## About the Project

The manufacturing of a specific steel grade for automotive applications did not allow on-time delivery to customers. The random and uncontrolled scrap rate caused significant production restarts from the beginning of the manufacturing line.

This project enabled a drastic reduction in the scrap rate through a redefinition of the line programming.

Simultaneously, post-production analysis still relied on manual processing. The requirement to track this steel grade was extended to all campaigns to understand, capitalize on, and prepare for the next one.

## Challenge

The project aimed to make campaign success less dependent on sporadic analyses or scattered knowledge.

Three complementary axes emerged from the work:

1. **better organize campaigns**, by positioning sensitive products at times compatible with the installation's condition;
2. **formalize programming constraints**, to transform experience feedback into rules usable by teams;
3. **structure post-campaign analysis**, so that anomalies, decisions, and lessons learned can feed the preparation of the next campaign.

## Approach

The first phase consisted of analyzing campaigns and aligning their execution with production events.

Benchmarking with a similar line allowed understanding the origin of scrap and proposing a campaign scheme. This scheme also included trial products and immediate qualification to verify if process conditions allowed committing the rest of the planned batch. This approach enabled teams not only to strive for necessary conditions (through actions and the ability to verify their effectiveness) but also to protect the batch and avoid committing it blindly.

These principles were subsequently integrated into the Programming Specification, which structures responsibilities, format constraints, transition rules, and the scheme of the different phases of a campaign.

The approach deliberately retained an empirical dimension. Subsequent analyses of campaigns showed that a configuration observed on certain campaigns could not be generalized without caution. This reservation is important: the objective was not to turn a correlation into an absolute rule, but to confront hypotheses with real campaigns.

A second aspect focused on capitalization: enabling everyone to produce a simple campaign report, identify anomalies to address, distinguish immediate actions from fundamental actions, standardize documents before, during, and after production, and share a common vision.

Finally, this logic was extended by a data project, which allowed aligning process data, product data, quality results, and inspection information to obtain campaign summaries and more systematic analyses.

## My Role

As Process & Quality Manager:
- Campaign analysis and benchmarking
- Proposal and implementation of the new campaign scheme
- Monitoring of preliminary trials and validation
- Integration of new constraints into the Programming Specification (in coordination with Supply Chain)
- Development, sharing, and dissemination of the Campaign Report (Power BI)

## Results and Lessons Learned

The new campaign scheme enabled multiplying the success volume by 6 for each campaign. Reprogramming and production restarts disappeared.

## Lessons Learned

> This project demonstrated that improving industrial performance relies not only on optimizing process parameters but also on how production is organized.
> 

Several lessons can be drawn from this:

- **Understand before optimizing.** Aligning production events, quality results, and practices from a comparable facility helped identify conditions favorable to successful campaigns.
- **Validate before committing.** Introducing trial products followed by immediate qualification allowed verification of process conditions before running a full batch. This approach transformed risky production into decisions based on observations.
- **Turn experience into operational rules.** Formalizing constraints in the programming specification enabled sustaining improvements and reducing reliance on individual knowledge.
- **Make every campaign a learning source.** Standardizing reviews and aligning process, product, and quality data helped structure lessons learned while maintaining a critical view on generalizing results.

> The central lesson is that sustainable performance is built by linking process knowledge, risk control, programming rules, and data exploitation within the same continuous improvement loop.
>
