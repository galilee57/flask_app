# Reducing continuous casting embedding defects through process and cooling control

## Context

In a continuous casting workshop, the increase in embedding defects reached a concerning level.

These defects had significant consequences on product quality, internal non-conformance costs, and the ability to meet customer delivery commitments.

The situation required a structured response to understand defect formation mechanisms and restore expected production standards.

I led a **cross-functional working group** bringing together the necessary skills for process analysis, facility observation, and implementation of corrective actions.

The objective was to address the technical causes of embedding, rather than limiting efforts to handling defective products.

## Objectives

- Restore a quality level compatible with production standards.
- Limit internal non-conformance costs.
- Identify process parameters contributing to defect occurrence.
- Implement technical and organizational modifications.
- Sustain results through production and maintenance standards.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Structure a cross-functional working group

In response to the increase in defects, I organized and led a collective problem-solving approach.

The working group gathered diverse competencies to examine possible causes of embedding.

This approach allowed cross-referencing production team observations, facility knowledge, and product quality analyses.

My role was to guide investigations, organize trials, and coordinate the implementation of solutions.

### 2. Observe the process on-site

I participated in observations of actual continuous casting conditions.

The objective was to compare operations practices and equipment performance with defects observed on products.

This step enabled directing investigations toward several key process parameters.

Field observations were an indispensable complement to the analysis of defective products.

### 3. Analyze defective products and organize trials

We conducted analyses of products with encrustations and performed trials to better understand the conditions under which defects appeared.

These works highlighted several factors contributing to quality drift:

- Insufficient control of casting temperatures.
- Insufficient control of casting speeds.
- Issues related to cooling water quality.

This analysis allowed us to move beyond initial hypotheses and focus actions on process parameters and cooling conditions.

### 4. Improve control of casting parameters

Based on investigation results, we implemented process modifications aimed at better controlling production conditions.

Actions focused on the identified parameters contributing to defects, particularly temperatures and casting speeds.

The objective was to restore more stable operating conditions compatible with quality requirements.

### 5. Adapt cleaning operations

Investigations also highlighted the importance of cooling water quality.

We revised cleaning schedules to better meet facility needs.

These adaptations aimed to limit conditions favoring encrustation and improve control over cooling.

This step showed that product quality depended as much on production parameters as on equipment and circuit maintenance.

### 6. Sustain improvements through standards

After implementing modifications, I led the locking of actions into production and maintenance practices.

The objective was to prevent improvements from relying solely on occasional team vigilance.

Standards enabled the integration of new practices into the usual operation of facilities.

This approach thus combined technical correction and prevention of recurrence.

## My Role

**I led the cross-functional working group tasked with understanding and reducing encrustation defects in continuous casting.**

I drove the initiative, mobilizing technical and operational skills from the concerned teams.

## Technologies and Methods

**Technical domains:** continuous casting, metallurgy, temperature control, casting speeds, industrial cooling, water quality, maintenance.

**Methods:** structured problem solving, multidisciplinary working group, field observations, industrial trials, defect analysis, action plan, standardization.

**Skills:** project management, process analysis, cross-functional management, continuous improvement, quality control, production-maintenance coordination.

## Results

The project enabled the following results:

- Identification of major deviations associated with scale defects.
- Highlighting the importance of temperatures and casting speeds.
- Reduction and control of scale defects, restoring a level compatible with production standards.
- Integration of improvements into production and maintenance standards.

## Lessons Learned

### 1. Maintenance directly contributes to product quality

The quality of cooling water and the efficiency of cleaning operations influence production conditions.

Therefore, preventing defects requires close coordination between production and maintenance.

### 2. An improvement is only sustainable when standardized

Correcting a defect is not enough.

Lessons must be translated into production and maintenance practices to limit the risk of recurrence.

**This project illustrates my ability to lead an end-to-end industrial problem-solving approach: mobilizing teams, understanding the root causes of quality deviations, conducting trials, implementing modifications, and securing results through standardization of practices.**
