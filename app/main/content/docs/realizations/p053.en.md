# Leveraging thickness gauge data for industrial product analysis and qualification

## About the Project

As part of integrating a new industrial line, a thickness measurement system was required to contribute to product qualification and monitoring of dimensional characteristics.

The solution relied on a gauge capable of performing different types of measurements, including multi-transverse position acquisitions and the construction of thickness profiles.

The challenge went beyond measurement itself: the information produced had to be integrated into industrial systems, structured, and made accessible for exploitation.

**I worked on retrieving, comparing, and leveraging data from industrial measurement systems to facilitate their interpretation and prepare additional analyses.**

## Objectives

My work aimed to:

- Finalize the project, ensure acceptance testing
- Define and implement maintainability (spare parts, calibration bench, standards)
- Exploit data generated by new thickness gauges.

## Approach

### 1. Understand measurement data characteristics

An industrial thickness gauge does not necessarily produce a single measurement per product.

Depending on the acquisition strategy, it can provide cyclic measurements, values associated with different transverse positions, and profiles describing thickness variations.

### 2. Retrieve and structure data

I worked on retrieving data from new gauges to make them exploitable.

Structuring information was a central challenge: a measurement only fully makes sense when it can be associated with its industrial context and compared with other relevant observations.

This step prepared the data for their representation and analysis.

### 3. Compare measurements

I leveraged available information to compare results from different measurement systems.

This approach allowed us to examine observed behaviors and highlight differences or similarities between the data.

The objective was to facilitate the interpretation of measurements, without automatically equating an observed deviation with a metrological defect.

### 4. Develop graphical representations

I built graphical representations intended to facilitate the reading of thickness data.

Visualization was a crucial step in moving from sets of values to information interpretable by users.

It notably allowed for preparing the analysis of measurement variations and comparisons between different acquisitions.

### 5. Prepare statistical exploitation

Beyond visualization, I identified the interest of extending data exploitation through statistical treatments.

This approach was based on an essential principle: **properly structured and accessible data open the possibility for more advanced analyses**.

Depending on business needs, these treatments can notably serve to study dispersion, stability, or the evolution of measured characteristics.

These possibilities represented exploitation prospects, without prejudging their effective deployment within the framework of this project.

## A multidisciplinary technical environment

The integration project for gauges combined several complementary domains.

**Metrology**

The system had to meet the measurement requirements necessary for product qualification. Acceptance tests notably included stability, noise, and repeatability checks.

**Automation and control**

The instrument had to communicate with line systems to receive instructions and transmit its measurements.

**Data integration**

Acquired values had to be available in industrial systems to enable their exploitation and use in product qualification.

**Business exploitation**

Measurement data had to be consultable, comparable, and analyzable to support industrial monitoring and quality activities.

My intervention was mainly situated on this last dimension, at the interface between measurement systems and data analysis.

## My role

I contributed to the exploitation of data from the new thickness gauges.

This contribution was part of a collective project to integrate measurement equipment, combining instrumentation, automation, industrial computing, metrology, and operations.

## Results

**A concrete approach to leveraging industrial measurement data.**

The main results include:

- Commissioning of the gauge
- Provision of a calibration bench and spare parts
- Exploitation of data from new thickness gauges.

## Lessons Learned

**The value of an industrial measurement instrument is not limited to its precision: it also depends on the ability to retrieve, structure, contextualize, and exploit the data it produces.**

**This project demonstrates my ability to bridge industrial instrumentation, information systems, and data exploitation in service of product quality.**
