# Designing and initiating an agentic AI solution to retro-document an industrial system

## About the Project

In an industrial environment, a legacy computer system applied complex technical rules developed in Java.

These rules were difficult for business users to understand and evaluate, as they did not have a sufficiently clear view of the mechanisms actually implemented.

Their analysis required bringing together source code, specifications, expert knowledge, and operational results.

This situation constituted a particularly interesting use case for generative artificial intelligence and agentic architectures.

**I imagined and initiated a project aimed at using AI agents to transform the technical rules of a legacy system into structured, understandable, and exploitable knowledge for business functions.**

## Objectives

The project pursued several objectives:

- Extract business rules contained in the Java code.
- Translate these rules into pseudocode and understandable language.
- Build a structured documentation base.
- Facilitate rule consultation through AI agents.
- Compare implemented rules with initial specifications.
- Bring rules closer to operational results.
- Prepare mechanisms for auditing and evolving rules.

The ambition was to build a solution capable of facilitating the understanding of complex software heritage, without requiring users to master its programming language.

## Approach

### 1. Identify a relevant use case for agentic AI

Drawing on my knowledge of generative artificial intelligence technologies, agents, and Microsoft Copilot Studio, I identified an opportunity to apply this to a concrete industrial problem.

Business rules existed in the code, but their understanding and maintenance were difficult.

I proposed using AI to facilitate their extraction, translation, and consultation.

This approach did not consist of adding a chatbot to an existing application, but rather rethinking how users could access the system's technical knowledge.

### 2. Imagine the functional architecture

I designed the principle of a solution articulating several components:

- The legacy Java system as the source of the rules actually implemented.
- A reverse-documentation chain transforming code into accessible representations.
- A structured documentation base.
- Agents developed with Microsoft Copilot Studio.
- Automations realized with Power Automate.
- Power BI indicators and dashboards.

This architecture aimed to bring technical knowledge, business specifications, and operational results closer together.

**My contribution consisted in defining the concept, expected use cases, and how the different technologies could be associated to address the industrial problem.**

### 3. Structure reverse-documentation

I contributed to defining a transformation chain:

**Java Code → Pseudocode → Business Documentation → AI Agents**

The pseudocode was intended to constitute an intermediate representation of the rules, independent of Java implementation details.

The idea of a common representation, or *lingua franca*, allowed for considering the comparison, documentation, and audit of the rules.

This structuring constituted an essential prerequisite for their exploitation by agents.

### 4. Guide technology choices

My knowledge of the Microsoft ecosystem and the possibilities offered by Copilot Studio enabled me to guide the design of the solution.

The approach associated:

**Microsoft Copilot Studio** for the agent dimension and interaction with knowledge.

**Power Automate** for automation mechanisms.

**Power BI** for the restitution and analysis of operational results.

The objective was to build a coherent device in which each technology addressed an identified functional need.

### 5. Materialize the concept in a prototype

Technical development was carried out by an intern dedicated to the project.

She notably developed the Copilot Studio agents, Power Automate flows, and Power BI reporting.

This collaboration enabled concretizing the proposed approach and producing a first prototype associating reverse-documentation, automation, and AI-assisted consultation.

The distinction between design and implementation is important: **I provided the idea, functional vision, and technology guidance; the intern ensured technical implementation of the components.**

### 6. Formalize and present the solution

I wrote a scoping documentation presenting the project objectives and the envisaged architecture.

I also presented the device during an exchange dedicated to artificial intelligence initiatives.

This presentation explained:

- The initial industrial problem.
- The interest of reverse documentation.
- The role of AI agents.
- The alignment between expected rules and implemented rules.
- The use of operational data to analyze results.
- The prospects for the prototype's evolution.

## My Role

**I initiated the project and its agent-based approach, providing the concept, technology knowledge, and functional design of the solution.**

My contributions included:

- Identifying the industrial use case.
- Proposing an approach based on generative AI and agents.
- Leveraging my knowledge of Microsoft Copilot Studio.
- Defining objectives and expected functionalities.
- Designing the functional architecture.
- Guiding technology choices.
- Formalizing the reverse documentation chain.
- Writing the project scope.
- Presenting the concept and prototype results.

**The technical implementation of agents, automations, and reporting was handled by an intern**, within this collective framework, with solutions and adoption developed alongside the domain expert (and internship supervisor).

## Results

The project resulted in a prototype combining:

- A reverse documentation approach for Java rules.
- Structured representation in pseudocode.
- More accessible documentation.
- Agents built with Copilot Studio.
- Power Automate flows.
- Power BI reporting.

The prototype demonstrated the value of an agent-based approach to facilitate access to complex business rules.

Analysis time was divided by 3.

## Lessons Learned

### Designing an agent-based solution goes beyond developing agents

It is necessary to understand knowledge sources, define interactions between components, and identify the functions intended for actual automation.

### AI requires structured knowledge

Agents become more useful when the information they access is explicit, organized, and verifiable.

### Domain expertise and technology knowledge are complementary

Understanding industrial problems enables proposing AI use cases that go beyond generic demonstrations.

**This first agent-based AI project demonstrates my ability to anticipate opportunities offered by new technologies, envision a concrete industrial application, and design an architecture combining AI agents, automation, and business data operations.**
