# Understanding surface defects through Machine Learning and process data analysis

## Context

On steel production lines, automatic inspection systems generate large quantities of images of surface defects.

The classifications produced by these systems sometimes group visually different defects within the same category. This heterogeneity complicates the identification of responsible mechanisms and the orientation of investigations.

**Objective: Identify homogeneous families of defects and link them to their location and manufacturing conditions to improve understanding of their origin.**

## Approach

I contributed to analysis approaches combining image processing, Machine Learning, and operations of industrial data.

They rely on three complementary steps.

**1. Identify visual families of defects**

- Constitution and preparation of image populations.
- Normalization of orientation, dimensions, and contrast.
- Extraction of visual features.
- Application of clustering algorithms to search for homogeneous groupings.
- Validation of obtained families with domain experts.

**2. Generalize defect recognition**

From the identified and validated families, construction of a classification model allowing extension of recognition to larger image populations.

This approach allows moving from exploratory analysis to more systematic processing of defects.

**3. Link defects to their industrial context**

Classification results are correlated with manufacturing information:

- Location of defects on products.
- Product genealogy through the various transformation steps.
- Conversion of coordinates between upstream and downstream products.
- Cross-referencing with process data and manufacturing conditions.

The objective is to identify characteristic distributions and search for industrial mechanisms likely to explain them.

## Technical Achievements

**Image Processing and Preparation**

Development and evaluation of treatments designed to make image populations comparable and to limit the influence of irrelevant features.

**Clustering and classification**

Search for homogeneous visual families, analysis of their coherence, and use of reference populations to train classifiers.

**Processing large populations**

Application of classification models to larger image sets to extend analyses beyond initial samples.

**Genealogy and defect localization**

Alignment of recognition results with product data and coordinate transformation to retrieve positions corresponding to different manufacturing stages.

**Analysis of industrial mechanisms**

Exploitation of spatial distributions and process data to guide investigations into the origin of defects.

## My Role

I contributed to the development and structuring of this analytical approach, at the interface between image processing, data analysis, and industrial process knowledge.

My work focused on:

- Preparation and analysis of image populations.
- Exploration of groupings via clustering.
- Evaluation of preprocessing and classification methods.
- Alignment of results with manufacturing data.
- Capitalization of methods and lessons learned.

Validation of defect families and their industrial interpretation were carried out in collaboration with domain experts.

## Results

- Identification of more homogeneous and interpretable visual defect families.
- Construction of analytical chains associating clustering and classification.
- Extension of analyses to larger image populations.
- Alignment of defects with their location and product genealogy.
- Identification of spatial distributions enabling guidance on research into defect formation mechanisms.

## Lessons Learned

Recognizing a defect is not enough to understand its origin: it is the alignment between its appearance, location, and manufacturing context that adds value to the analysis.

Three lessons structure this approach:

Prepare without distorting: image transformations must be evaluated experimentally, as the visual context around a defect may contain useful information.

Confront clusters with industrial reality: mathematical proximity between images does not guarantee the existence of a relevant defect family for the process.

Link Machine Learning to process physics: product genealogy, defect locations, and process data enable transforming visual groupings into industrial investigation leads.

> Machine Learning serves here as an exploration and diagnostic aid tool, whose value depends equally on data quality and domain knowledge as on the algorithms used.
>
