# Model the load and capacity of a heat treatment workshop to inform industrial decisions

## Context

The alignment between commercial forecasts and industrial capacities is a major issue for production planning and investment decisions.

In a heat treatment workshop, available capacity does not depend solely on the tonnage to be treated. It also varies according to the nature of the treatments, their duration, rearrangement operations, and equipment availability.

The increased quality demands from clients in the oil and gas sector necessitated additional operations within manufacturing processes. The load distribution was disrupted, creating intermittent and mobile bottlenecks.

**Objective: model the workshop load** to redefine new processes and, if necessary, divert capacity externally.

## Approach

I conducted a load-capacity analysis by comparing commercial forecasts with the operational characteristics of the workshop.

My work was organized around five axes.

### 1. Translate commercial forecasts into industrial load

I utilized forecasted volumes to assess the treatment needs of the workshop.

This analysis required distinguishing between different families of treatments, whose durations and equipment usage constraints are not identical.

I developed calculations to align:

- The forecasted tonnages to be treated.
- The distribution among treatment families.
- The associated durations and constraints.
- Rearrangement needs.
- The capacities of available or planned equipment.

This approach allowed moving beyond a global comparison of tonnages to reason in terms of actual load on industrial resources.

### 2. Build an initial capacity scenario

I established a first simulation based on assumptions, including:

- The proportion of long-duration treatments.
- The rearrangement rate.
- The availability of a new treatment cell.
- The distribution of load among equipment.

**This first scenario revealed a capacity deficit** under the conditions retained.

This result led to a deeper analysis of the assumptions, rather than immediately considering the calculated deficit as an inevitable situation.

### 3. Identify the most sensitive parameters

I examined the influence of the assumptions on the calculated load.

The proportion of long treatments emerged as a particularly determining parameter: a variation in this distribution could significantly alter the assessment of the required capacity.

I therefore questioned the representativeness of this assumption by confronting it with available production data.

This sensitivity analysis allowed distinguishing between the workshop's structural constraints and effects related to simulation assumptions.

### 4. Confront scenarios with production data

To deepen this analysis, I examined historical production tonnages by applying filters on certain customer scopes.

This work enabled me to discuss the actual distribution of treatments and build an alternative scenario based on a lower proportion of long treatments.

I then compared the obtained results with the order of magnitude observed during certain production periods.

**The goal was to make the assumptions more representative of industrial conditions, while identifying points requiring further validation.**

### 5. Identify bottlenecks and evaluate capacity evolution

I distinguished treatment families according to their level of constraint on equipment and examined the corresponding monthly loads.

The analysis also covered the potential effect of new treatment cells and investment proposals.

## My role

I personally performed the load-capacity calculations and simulations, then presented the results and associated assumptions for discussion.

My contributions covered:

- Analysis of forecast commercial volumes.
- Modeling of heat treatment loads.
- Construction of capacity scenarios.
- Sensitivity analysis of production parameters.
- Exploitation of historical data to discuss assumptions.
- Identification of constraining equipment.
- Evaluation of possible consequences of capacity additions.
- Presentation of results for industrial exchanges and arbitrations.

This work combined data analysis, knowledge of the industrial process, and modeling of production scenarios.

## Results

- **Construction of several load-capacity scenarios** for the heat treatment workshop.
- Highlighting a capacity deficit in the initial scenario.
- Identification of the proportion of long treatments as a particularly sensitive parameter.
- Search for external capacities
- Investments to debottleneck
- Redefinition of the site's commercial strategy taking these constraints into account.

## Lessons learned

**An industrial capacity study should not be limited to comparing volumes: it must model process constraints and make explicit the assumptions that determine the result.**

Three principles emerge from this experience:

- **Think in terms of load rather than tonnage**: two identical volumes can mobilize equipment very differently depending on the required treatments.
- **Test hypothesis sensitivity**: a calculated capacity deficit must be analyzed in light of the parameters that determine it.
- **Think at the industrial system scale**: increasing the capacity of one piece of equipment may simply shift the bottleneck to another step in the process.

**This project illustrates my ability to build industrial load models, leverage production data, and provide quantified elements to inform planning and investment decisions.**
