# Debottlenecking a heat treatment workshop by mobilizing the capacities of other plants

## Context

As head of department of a heat treatment workshop, I was responsible for production volumes and participated in the production development strategy.

The evolution of customer requirements had gradually changed processing needs and created bottlenecks on certain workshop capacities.

Available facilities were no longer able to respond with the same flexibility to all requests.

The issue was not limited to a global capacity shortage: it concerned the distribution of loads between different furnace families and their ability to process products according to customer requirements.

Rather than immediately considering an investment in new equipment, we studied the possibility of mobilizing available capacities in other plants.

This approach aimed to **shift the load from saturated equipment to more numerous furnace families**, while preserving quality requirements and controlling logistical constraints.

## Objectives

- Increase the effective capacity of the workshop.
- Identify bottlenecks limiting production volumes.
- Seek debottlenecking solutions on-site.
- Exploit available capacities in other plants.
- Verify the technical capability of alternative furnaces.
- Obtain necessary approvals for concerned products and customers.
- Organize logistical flows between sites.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Identify the workshop's capacity constraints

I participated in analyzing the workshop's capacities to identify equipment and furnace families constituting the main bottlenecks.

The challenge was to understand the relationships between product processing needs, available capacities, and technical constraints of installations.

This analysis allowed distinguishing saturations that could be resolved locally from those requiring a solution at the scale of multiple sites.

### 2. Seek debottlenecking possibilities on-site

We first examined possibilities for improving the utilization of existing equipment.

The objective was to identify margins for maneuver allowing better distribution of load between different furnace families.

This reflection contributed to defining needs that could not be sufficiently absorbed by local capacities alone.

### 3. Identify available capacities in other plants

I participated in the search for heat treatment solutions at other industrial sites.

This approach aimed to align our workshop's production needs with available capacities elsewhere.

It was necessary to take into account furnace characteristics, required treatments, and quality requirements specific to the products concerned.

**The principle was to reason at the scale of all available industrial means, rather than limiting the search for solutions to my department's scope.**

### 4. Organize industrial capability trials

The availability of a furnace was not sufficient to guarantee it could process products under the required conditions.

We therefore organized actual trials to demonstrate that alternative installations could achieve the expected quality.

These trials constituted an essential step to secure the transfer of treatments.

They allowed confronting the theoretical possibilities of equipment with their industrial results.

### 5. Obtain necessary approvals

Some products were subject to specific customer requirements, mandating approval of the installations used.

The **Quality Service** took charge of the necessary validations and approvals.

This step ensured that treatments performed at other sites met applicable requirements.

The approach thus combined capacity search with indispensable technical and quality validation.

### 6. Organize inter-site logistics

Resorting to other plants also required organizing product flows between installations.

The **Supply Chain** took charge of the logistical aspects associated with this new organization.

It was necessary to integrate site-to-site transfers into industrial operations, taking into account production constraints and customer needs.

This coordination was essential to transform available capacities into a truly exploitable solution.

### 7. Support change between sites

Searching for capacities in other plants also raised organizational questions.

Transferring volumes could generate resistance, particularly when perceived as a loss of activity for a site.

I was confronted with the necessity to explain the overall logic of the approach and move beyond local interests.

The objective was not to put sites in competition, but to better utilize available industrial capacities to meet customer needs.

This approach required building a shared understanding of the challenges and leveraging the contributions of different teams.

## My Role

**As department head, responsible for production volumes, I participated in defining and implementing this industrial debottlenecking strategy.**

The project relied on cross-site and cross-functional collaboration.

Approvals were handled by the Quality Service, while logistics organization was managed by Supply Chain.

## Technologies and Methods

**Areas:** heat treatment, industrial capacity, production planning, process qualification, customer approval, cross-site logistics.

**Methods:** bottleneck analysis, capacity search, industrial trials, capability validation, cross-site coordination, change management.

**Skills:** industrial strategy, production management, capacity optimization, cross-functional steering, cost control, negotiation and change support.

## Results

### Increased Available Capacity

Mobilizing facilities from other plants enabled debottlenecking the workshop and provided additional treatment capacities.

### No Investment Required

**The capacity increase was achieved without investing in new equipment.**

Using existing industrial means allowed avoiding a solution requiring capital expenditure.

### Increased Flexibility

Managing transferred treatments via variable costs provided greater flexibility to adjust volumes to evolving customer demand.

### Qualification of Alternative Solutions

Industrial trials and necessary approvals secured the use of alternative capacities for the affected products.

### Site Cooperation

The project mobilized multiple plants as well as Production, Quality, and Supply Chain functions around a common solution.

## Lessons Learned

### 1. A production bottleneck must be analyzed at the industrial system level.

Equipment saturation does not necessarily mean that the company lacks overall capacity.

Other facilities may have available margins, provided their compatibility with requirements is verified.

### 2. The best additional capacity is sometimes what already exists

Before investing in new means, it is essential to examine the possibilities offered by existing installations at other sites.

This approach can increase capacity while maintaining a more flexible cost structure.

### 3. A technically feasible solution must be industrially validated

Capacity searches cannot be limited to comparing equipment characteristics.

Actual trials and customer approvals are indispensable to demonstrate the ability to achieve required quality.

### 4. Logistics is an integral part of production capacity

An available installation at another plant becomes an operational solution only if product flows can be organized coherently.

### 5. Resistance to change often outweighs technical issues

Transferring volumes between sites may be perceived as a threat to local operations.

It is essential to explain objectives, acknowledge concerns, and build a shared vision.

**This project illustrates my ability to contribute to an industrial strategy extending beyond my department's scope: identifying production constraints, seeking cross-site solutions, demonstrating their technical feasibility, coordinating their implementation, and supporting change management to increase capacity without investment.**
