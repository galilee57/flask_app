# Industrializing data exploitation in a Big Data environment

## About the Project

In a complex industrial environment, production, process, quality, and traceability data are distributed across numerous systems.

This fragmentation limits cross-functional analysis possibilities, complicates the reconstruction of manufacturing histories, and requires specific extractions for each new requirement.

The deployment of a Big Data platform represented a major transformation: it became possible to gradually centralize this data in a common environment, preserve its history, and exploit it for reporting, industrial analysis, and artificial intelligence applications.

**I actively participated in this transformation by combining my knowledge of industrial processes with operational practice of Big Data technologies, notably PySpark.**

My intervention aimed to bridge the gap between the technical availability of data and its effective exploitation by users.

## Objectives

My contribution was structured around four objectives:

- Identify and prioritize industrial data of interest to business units.
- Support their progressive integration into the Big Data cluster.
- Develop processing methods to transform raw data into usable information.
- Facilitate access to data and its adoption by industrial teams.

The challenge was to go beyond simple data accumulation in a cluster to build genuine exploitation capabilities.

## Approach

### 1. Participating in the structuring of ingestion requirements

I contributed to identifying industrial data sources to be integrated and formalizing business unit requirements.

This approach required understanding:

- The systems and installations at the origin of the data.
- The nature of available information.
- History and traceability needs.
- Feasible industrial use cases.
- Integration priorities in light of operational needs.

I participated in prioritization exchanges and formulated proposals regarding different data scopes.

This contribution allowed aligning the technical ingestion roadmap with users' real expectations.

### 2. Understanding and exploiting the Data Lake architecture

The platform was built on a data organization structured across multiple transformation levels.

**RAW — Raw Data**

Data from source systems were integrated into the cluster, notably through streams relying on Kafka.

This zone retained information in a structure close to their original form.

**Trusted — Trusted Data**

The processing in this layer aimed to normalize, clean, and consolidate data to facilitate their reuse.

**Refined — Business-Oriented Data**

This layer was intended to expose datasets prepared for analytics, reporting, and industrial applications.

I worked directly with the available data in this environment, particularly to explore its structure, understand its specificities, and develop tailored processing logic.

### 3. Develop Generic Processing with PySpark

One of my technical contributions consisted in developing and sharing a generic data processing script for an industrial installation.

The goal was to be able to process a large number of tables without having to manually rebuild a specific processing logic for each one.

This approach required taking into account the heterogeneity of data structures and producing reusable results.

**The script enabled processing the majority of tables within the studied scope and made the data available in exploitable formats.**

This experimentation also allowed identifying several technical limitations:

- Tables present but not populated.
- Specific structures requiring custom processing.
- Schema differences between sources.
- The need to evolve towards incremental processing to avoid systematically reprocessing all data.

Beyond the initial use case, this work contributed to identifying the conditions necessary for more industrialized data exploitation.

### 4. Facilitate Data Access for Business Units

One of the major challenges of the program was to enable industrial users to leverage the data available in the cluster.

I contributed to this adoption by sharing methods, scripts, and concrete examples of data exploitation.

This approach included:

- Workshops dedicated to data access.
- Sharing PySpark processing examples.
- Supporting colleagues in discovering the cluster.
- Exploring the possibilities offered by KNIME.
- Formalizing requirements for interfaces more accessible to users not practicing programming.

**My goal was to reduce the distance between Big Data infrastructure and business users, so that the platform became a genuine working tool.**

### 5. Contribute to Improving Industrialization Practices

The operations of the cluster highlighted several challenges extending beyond the development of initial data flows:

- Documentation of data and their schemas.
- Identification of the sources actually being populated.
- Standardization of processing.
- Monitoring of data quality and availability.
- Control of volumes and performance.
- Preparation of incremental processing.
- Clarification of responsibilities between technical teams and business units.

These topics informed my reflection on the conditions necessary to transition from an environment containing data to a platform offering truly usable data.

## My Role

I held a cross-functional role combining **industrial expertise, PySpark development, and user support**.

My main contributions included:

- Formalizing business requirements for ingesting industrial data.
- Participating in the prioritization of sources to be integrated.
- Technical exploration of data present in the cluster.
- Developing and sharing a generic PySpark script.
- Identifying difficulties related to schemas and data availability.
- Proposing improvement paths for table operations.
- Leading and participating in workshops for data adoption.
- Supporting users in transitioning to PySpark and KNIME.

I operated at the interface between industrial teams and platform specialists, without substituting myself for the teams responsible for architecture, infrastructure, or ingestion pipelines.

## Results

**A concrete contribution to transforming industrial data into exploitable resources.**

The main results include:

- Participation in structuring ingestion requirements and priorities.
- Effective exploitation of industrial data available in the cluster.
- Development of a generic PySpark processing applicable to the majority of tables within a studied scope.
- Provision of results in reusable formats.
- Identification of technical limitations and industrialization paths.
- Sharing of scripts and methods with other users.
- Contribution to upskilling business teams on Big Data tools.

At the program level, several industrial scopes were integrated into the cluster, and real use cases began exploiting the available data.

Normalization and self-service availability remain areas for improvement.

## Lessons Learned

**Ingestion is only the first step in valorizing industrial data. True transformation begins when business units can understand, manipulate, and exploit these data autonomously.**

This experience reinforced several convictions.

**Industrial knowledge is indispensable for Data Engineering.** Understanding installations, production events, and data relationships helps avoid technically correct but irrelevant processing for users.

**Generality is a lever for industrialization.** Reusable processing reduces the multiplication of custom developments and facilitates the exploitation of new scopes.

**Data quality must be addressed from the moment of availability.** Tables not being populated, heterogeneous schemas, or insufficient documentation can severely limit the use of a platform that is technically operational.

**Supporting users is as important as technology.** Workshops, examples, and accessible tools allow transforming specialized infrastructure into a shared resource.

**This project illustrates my ability to intervene concretely in an industrial Big Data environment, develop processing with PySpark, and support business units in adopting new data exploitation methods.**
