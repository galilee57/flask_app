# Digitalization and automation of an operational control with Microsoft 365

## Situation
In an industrial environment, an operational control had to be performed regularly in the cabin. The documented objectives were to digitalize its execution, better exploit the remarks raised by operators to improve responsiveness, and make the process more robust.

The solution relies on several Microsoft 365 building blocks. Microsoft Forms ensures data entry for controls.  
The responses feed an Excel database via Power Automate.   
The information is then transformed and distributed to operational managers and management, while a Power BI reporting tool ensures monitoring.

## Challenge

The challenge was not only to replace manual entry with a digital form.

The device had to create continuity between:

1. the execution of the control;
2. the collection of information;
3. their automated processing;
4. their distribution to management;
5. their consolidation in a monitoring tool.

The Power BI reporting thus relies on the data used by the automation to extend the control with operational monitoring.

## Approach

The process revolves around several automations.

- When a form is validated, a processing step feeds the database used by the device. 
- Flows associated with different workstations then build the information intended for managers. 
- Another processing step ensures the daily cleaning of controls already processed. 
- Finally, an automation establishes a weekly balance between performed and planned controls.

The functional architecture can be summarized as follows:

**Microsoft Forms → Power Automate → database → emails to management → Power BI**

The device therefore includes several complementary levels:

- **field collection** with Microsoft Forms;
- **centralization of responses**; 
- **automation of processing** with Power Automate;
- **regular notification of management**; 
- **reporting and monitoring** with Power BI.

A follow-up exchange confirms that the device continued to automatically collect forms, transmit a summary to management, and feed a dashboard.

## My Role

Direct technical contribution to the automation and its operational maintenance: I notably described **and developed** the flows, monitored their operation, and proposed sharing the technical implementation to explain how they work or to enable evolutions.

The exchanges also show a separation between technical responsibility and business responsibility. When the use of another solution raised the question of maintaining existing automation, I requested the agreement of the concerned managers before stopping the flows.

This approach allows business responsibility for the decision to be retained while ensuring the technical maintenance of the device.

## Collective Contribution

Controls are driven by frontline users. The information generated serves operational managers and leadership. Business owners also intervene in decisions regarding the maintenance or evolution of the device.

## Results

More than **32,000 responses** over a period of **six years**, with near-continuous operation.

## Lessons Learned

This project demonstrates that a low-code architecture based on standard tools can go beyond the prototype stage and sustainably support an operational process.

It also highlights a significant limitation: the functional success of a business automation is not sufficient to guarantee its full industrialization.

> However, it is not necessary to wait for the perfect IT solution before starting: a good prototype is better than a potential project!
> 

When a device becomes sustainable or important for operations, additional topics emerge:

- governance;
- documentation · maintainability;
- error management;
- capacity for recovery by an IT team.
