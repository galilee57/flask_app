# Apply Machine Learning to over 30 industrial quality issues

## About this experience

In industry, the occurrence of a quality defect on a production line does not necessarily mean its origin lies in that same facility.

A defect may result from manufacturing parameters located several steps upstream, complex interactions between variables, or sporadic phenomena difficult to identify with classical methods.

Identifying causes then requires reconstructing the product history, cross-referencing numerous data sources, and understanding the physical mechanisms of the process.

**I developed expertise combining Data Science, Machine Learning, and in-depth knowledge of industrial processes, which I mobilized in over 30 use cases.**

These works focused on different families of defects: coupling marks, wrinkles, contamination, rust, PickUps, flatness defects, and issues related to annealing processes.

## Objectives

My approach aims to use industrial data to accelerate and deepen quality investigations.

It pursues several objectives:

- Identify parameters that differentiate conforming and non-conforming products.
- Explore relationships between manufacturing conditions and observed defects.
- Detect atypical behaviors or sporadic phenomena.
- Reduce the number of hypotheses to investigate.
- Reconstruct manufacturing conditions across multiple facilities.
- Provide industrial experts with interpretable statistical results.
- Capitalize on analysis methods reusable for different problems.

**Machine Learning constitutes here a tool for scientific and industrial investigation, not a black box automatically producing root causes.**

## Approach

### 1. Define the industrial problem

Each study begins with a precise understanding of the defect and its context of occurrence.

With quality and process teams, I work to define populations to analyze, relevant periods, and initial technical hypotheses.

The distinction between conforming (OK) and non-conforming (NOK) products often constitutes the starting point of the analysis.

I systematically initiate a shared Microsoft Loop file to collect, clarify, and capitalize on each study.

This step is essential: the quality of the diagnosis depends as much on the relevance of the studied populations as on the choice of the algorithm.

### 2. Reconstruct the data and product genealogy

I collect and reconcile information from various manufacturing stages to reconstruct the product history.

Depending on the case, the treatments include:

- Joins between heterogeneous sources.
- Reconstruction of industrial genealogy.
- Data cleaning and normalization.
- Management of missing values.
- Selection and transformation of variables.
- Reconstruction of temporal sequences.
- Development of new explanatory variables (*feature engineering*).

This preparation enables the creation of consistent datasets, linking product characteristics to manufacturing conditions.

**My understanding of the process allows me to orient data construction towards physical mechanisms capable of explaining defects.**

### 3. Statistically explore populations

Before training models, I conduct an in-depth statistical exploration.

I notably use:

- Descriptive statistics.
- Distribution analysis.
- Comparison of OK/NOK populations.
- Hypothesis tests.
- Correlation analysis.
- Temporal representations.
- Search for discriminant parameters.

This step allows verifying data consistency, identifying significant differences, and preparing for modeling.

### 4. Apply Machine Learning methods

I select Machine Learning approaches based on the nature of the problem, data structure, and investigation objectives.

The mobilized methods include notably:

**Supervised learning**

Use of classification models and decision trees to differentiate populations and identify variables associated with defects.

**Unsupervised learning**

Use of clustering methods to search for groups of behaviors and identify structures not immediately visible in the data.

**Dimensionality reduction and variable selection**

Search for more synthetic representations to concentrate investigations on the most informative parameters.

**Feature engineering**

Construction of variables describing industrial phenomena more faithfully: histories, sequences, deviations, evolutions, and interactions between parameters.

Method selection is guided by their ability to produce results useful for industrial analysis, not just by their predictive performance.

### 5. Use Python and No-Code environments

I deploy different environments based on the study's needs.

**Python** is a central tool for developing custom processing, preparing data, and experimenting with models.

I have in-depth knowledge of the following libraries:

- **Scikit-learn**, for classical Machine Learning methods and modeling pipelines.
- **TensorFlow**, for Deep Learning approaches.
- **PyTorch**, for developing and experimenting with deep learning models.

I also use **Orange and KNIME** to build visual workflows, quickly explore different approaches, and facilitate sharing analyses with business stakeholders.

This complementarity allows me to choose between Python development and No-Code tools based on problem complexity, customization needs, and reusability conditions.

### 6. Confront results, make recommendations, and monitor over time

I systematically confront analytical results with:

- The chronology of events.
- The product genealogy.
- The physical conditions of the process.
- Field observations.
- Experts' hypotheses.
- Other available parameters.

**My industrial expertise enables me to distinguish an interesting statistical correlation from a technically plausible hypothesis, then guide the necessary investigations for its validation.**

## Use Case Examples

### Brands — Variable Reduction and Clustering

I built a multivariate database from available industrial data, then worked to reduce the number of studied parameters.

Statistical analysis and clustering enabled exploring differences between populations and focusing investigations on the most relevant variables.

### Grips — Multivariate Analysis and Temporal Reconstruction

This study combined several methods:

- Decision trees.
- Temporal analyses.
- Dimensionality reduction.
- Feature engineering.
- Parameter sequence reconstruction.
- KNIME workflows.

Crossing these approaches allowed emerging investigation leads related to process conditions.

### Flatness Defects — Statistical Tests and Physical Hypotheses

I compared product populations and used hypothesis tests to identify parameters showing significant differences.

Results allowed retaining certain process steps among hypotheses that could explain the appearance of internal constraints.

I also worked on improving the robustness and reproducibility of the KNIME workflow.

### Rust — Decision Trees and Identification of Discriminant Factors

Using a decision tree allowed emerging several process parameters associated with the studied populations.

Interpreting these results in the context of industrial operations helped target investigations.

### Annealing Line — Product Genealogy and Critical Model Analysis

This study particularly illustrates the importance of industrial knowledge in using Machine Learning.

I linked data from multiple manufacturing steps thanks to product genealogy.

A decision tree identified two discriminant parameters.

However, temporal analysis showed that these variables were insufficient to explain the occurrence of NOK products.

Rather than prematurely retaining these parameters as root causes, the investigation was redirected towards the hypothesis of a sporadic phenomenon.

**This case demonstrates that a statistically discriminant result does not necessarily constitute a causal explanation.**

My intervention covers, among other things:

## Results

**More than 30 studies and use cases of Data Science and Machine Learning applied to industrial problems.**

These studies enabled reducing rejection rates related to the analyzed defects.

## Lessons Learned

The power of industrial Machine Learning lies in the association of three expertise areas: understanding the process, mastering data, and the ability to interpret models.

>Data tell nothing! **You must make them speak …**  
> 

**Storytelling and visualization enable giving meaning.** Clearly defining the problem, sharing the analysis with operations staff, locking in each hypothesis, and confronting the analysis with local experts' opinions allows them to become active participants in the approach.

**These 30 use cases illustrate my operational mastery of Data Science and Machine Learning, combined with in-depth knowledge of industrial processes and the ability to lead complex investigations on production data.**
