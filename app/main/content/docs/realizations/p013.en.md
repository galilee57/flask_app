# Optimize rolling passes through industrial measurement and numerical simulation

## Context

As a process engineer in a rolling mill, I worked on optimizing rolling sequences, also called *rolling descents*.

Rolling progressively transforms the product section through successive imposed deformations between rolls.

How these deformations are distributed influences not only final dimensions but also mechanical stresses within the metal and, consequently, pore closure.

The project started from an observation: **for the same intermediate section, multiple rolling sequences could coexist**.

This diversity raised questions regarding rationalization, productivity, and metallurgical quality.

The challenge was to determine whether a different distribution of deformations could favor product compactness while respecting equipment limits and surface quality constraints.

To address this issue, we combined analysis of industrial calibrations with numerical simulation work carried out in collaboration with Research and Development.

## Objectives

- Improve metal compactness and favor pore closure at the core.
- Study the influence of deformation distribution between passes.
- Identify sequences offering favorable deformation conditions.
- Rationalize, where possible, sequences used for the same section.
- Verify compatibility of modifications with mill capabilities.
- Validate hypotheses through simulation and observation of industrial results.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Reconstruct existing rolling sequences

I began with a characterization of the process.

I reconstructed the rolling sequences used and performed dimensional measurements of the product at various stages of rolling.

This step allowed for an accurate representation of successive section transformations.

It also served as a common basis to compare different sequences and prepare simulation work.

### 2. Identify sequences favorable to compactness

Based on sequence analysis, I identified certain sequences likely to concentrate more deformation at the product core.

The hypothesis was that an appropriate distribution of deformations could create more favorable conditions for closing internal porosities.

Certain passes exhibiting interesting characteristics were selected as references to further investigate this hypothesis.

This initial selection was based on process knowledge, existing calibrations, and industrial measurements.

### 3. Validate the hypothesis with R&D through simulation

I worked with the Research and Development teams, who carried out **finite element numerical simulations**.

These simulations aimed to better understand the distribution of deformations in the product cross-section and examine conditions favorable to porosity closure.

The reference passes identified on-site were used to compare model results with known industrial conditions.

This work allowed validation of the studied hypothesis on these sequences and provided a sufficiently reliable model to continue investigations.

**My contribution focused on process characterization, measurements, and identification of reference passes; finite element modeling was performed by R&D.**

### 4. Extend the analysis to other rolling sequences

After this first step, we studied the possibility of applying lessons learned to other passes.

The objective was to seek a better distribution of deformations without degrading other process performances.

This extension required simultaneously examining internal compactness, surface quality, product dimensions, and industrial feasibility.

Optimization could therefore not be conducted on a single criterion.

### 5. Integrate the real constraints of the mill

The envisaged modifications had to remain compatible with the installation limits.

Several constraints were identified:

- **Motor capacity**, which limits admissible forces and torques.
- **Product engagement conditions**, critical for pass feasibility.
- **Groove geometry**, which determines achievable deformations.
- **Surface quality**, which must not be degraded by the new sequences.

The study notably considered bringing measured motor intensities closer to calculated torques for each pass.

This approach aimed to better link theoretical results with the mechanical and energy capabilities of the equipment.

### 6. Implement targeted modifications

Given the identified constraints, certain passes were modified on selected stands.

Changes were defined by seeking a compromise between improved internal deformation conditions and process feasibility.

The objective was not to systematically generalize a new sequence, but to apply modifications where they were technically relevant.

### 7. Verify results on the product

Products resulting from modifications were examined on the test bench.

**A reduction in core porosity was observed.**

This industrial observation constituted an essential result of the project: it allowed linking hypotheses on deformation distribution to an effectively observed improvement in internal quality.

## My role

**As a process engineer, I contributed to the technical definition and implementation of rolling optimizations, bridging industrial conditions and R&D simulation work.**

Numerical finite element simulations were carried out by the Research and Development teams.

## Technologies and methods

**Technical domains:** hot rolling, sizing, rolling schedules, plastic deformation, metallurgical compactness, internal porosity, grooves, motorization.

**Methods:** dimensional measurements, analysis of pass sequences, comparison of sizings, finite element simulation, model-reality confrontation, industrial trials, product inspection.

**Skills:** process engineering, physical analysis, multi-criteria optimization, collaboration with R&D, experimental validation, industrial problem solving.

## Results

### Identification of reference sequences

Analysis of rolling schedules allowed identifying configurations presenting deformation conditions interesting for pore closure.

### Validation of the hypothesis by simulation

R&D work confirmed the interest of the hypothesis on the studied schedules and allowed having a usable model to continue the analysis.

### Industrial implementation

Modifications were made to certain rolling passes, respecting the identified technical constraints.

### Improvement of internal quality

**A reduction in core porosity was observed on the test bench.**

## Lessons learned

### 1. Process settings must be linked to the physical mechanisms sought

Modifying a rolling schedule only makes sense if one understands its effect on the material.

### 2. Simulation is more relevant when it relies on industrial references

Existing measurements and drops provided a concrete basis for the modeling work.

### 3. Industrial optimization is necessarily multi-criteria

A solution favorable to internal compactness may encounter limits related to the motor, product engagement, grooves, or surface quality.


### 4. A targeted modification can be more relevant than a general redesign

Technical constraints did not necessarily allow modifying all sequences.

Applying improvements to certain passes nevertheless yielded a positive result on internal quality.

**This project illustrates my ability to link the physical analysis of a process, industrial measurements, and numerical simulation work to build and implement concrete improvements to metallurgical quality.**
