# Operations Manager at Nuclear Power Plant

## About the Experience

This stage of my professional career marks an important transition: acquiring the skills and qualifications necessary to perform the role of Operations Manager at a nuclear power plant, up to obtaining the nuclear safety authorization.

Beyond technical mastery, this experience exposed me to new requirements in terms of responsibility, management, and decision-making in an industrial environment where safety is an absolute priority.

## Job Context and Scope of Function

The role of **Operations Manager (OM)** constitutes an operational pivot for the operation of a nuclear reactor unit, within an organization operating continuously (3x8 shifts).

This position is exercised in a highly regulated environment, under the control of the Nuclear and Radioprotection Safety Authority (ASNR, formerly ASN), and requires permanent mastery of technical, human, and organizational issues.

- **Nuclear safety responsibility:** Ensure compliance with Technical Operation Specifications (TOS), risk control, and preservation of the three confinement barriers.
- **Operation management:** Supervise operation activities, coordinate operational maneuvers, periodic tests, and transient phases, including unit shutdowns and restarts.
- **Shift team management:** Lead/coordinate shift teams, organize activities, and ensure coordination with various plant functions (maintenance, radioprotection, chemistry, etc.).
- **Management of incidental and accidental situations:** Pilot operational response to incidents, enforce adapted procedures, make necessary decisions, and contribute to mobilizing the crisis organization.

## Regulatory Prerequisites and Authorization Process

Access to the Operations Manager role relies on a particularly demanding professional development path, combining knowledge acquisition, scenario-based training, and successive evaluations.

- **Theoretical and practical training:** Deepening knowledge related to Pressurized Water Reactor (PWR) operation, neutronics, thermohydraulics, and nuclear safety principles.
- **Full-scale simulator training:** Confrontation with normal, incidental, and accidental situations to develop analysis, decision-making, communication, and coordination capabilities under constraint.
- **Evaluation and authorization:** Validation of technical, behavioral, and managerial skills before an authorization jury, a prerequisite for issuing the authorization by the unit management.

## Lessons Learned and Experience Feedback

**Accepting to start from scratch and questioning one's achievements**

This professional transition led me to reconsider my previous experience and adopt a learner mindset. Despite the skills acquired throughout my career, I had to accept rebuilding legitimacy in a new environment with its own requirements and codes.

The authorization process, whose outcome relies partly on the jury's assessment, is also a particular experience: that of seeing several months of investment and a new professional orientation subjected to a decisive evaluation.

> I retain the importance of humility, perseverance, and the ability to question oneself, even after several years of experience.
> 

**Managing a team in highly diverse operational situations**

Managing a shift team is a particularly enriching human experience. It involves constantly adapting to encountered situations, whether it concerns normal facility operation, complex technical phases, or unforeseen events.

> This diversity taught me to adjust my managerial stance, build trust within the team, and balance operational demands, listening, and decision-making.
It also confirmed to me that collective performance relies as much on the quality of human relationships as on technical mastery.
> 

**Building an effective partnership with your deputy**

A particularly striking lesson concerns the operation of the team formed by the operations manager and their deputy.

Complementarity is not decreed: it is built through a clear definition of roles, responsibilities, and positioning for each. This clarification is essential to avoid ambiguities, ensure decision consistency, and establish a relationship of trust.

> The team quickly perceives the quality of this operation. An aligned, cohesive, and responsible partnership constitutes an essential factor for stability, managerial credibility, and operational efficiency.
> 

**In conclusion**, this experience taught me that exercising responsibilities in a high-stakes environment does not rely solely on technical expertise. It also requires knowing how to question one's certainties, build recognized authority, and create the conditions for sustainable collective performance.
