# Production Manager - Department Head

## About the experience

15 years of operational management in industrial production, on high-stakes installations: oil and gas sector, nuclear production, gas production, and steelmaking.
Responsibility for field teams, installation performance, and compliance with safety/security/quality standards specific to each industry.

## Missions and responsibilities
- Lead daily production: maintain schedules, coordinate teams, prioritize decisions.
- Ensure HSE compliance and operational permits within the departmental scope.
- Drive continuous improvement: analyze gaps, action plans, track indicators (availability, yield, costs).
- Supervise, train, and upskill production teams and frontline management.
- Manage degraded situations: incidents, unplanned stoppages, coordinate with maintenance and support.

## The Results

- Production performance:
 - TRG-driven management +25% workshop productivity
 - Reduce unplanned stoppages: identify bottlenecks, improve maintenance procedures, schedule short shutdowns
 - Reduce bottlenecks: using the SAP PM module, develop a production model (Constraint Management), identify bottlenecks and redirect production 
- Management and organization
 - Supervised workforce: 250 people
 - CHSCT President
 - Reduced turnover or absenteeism within the department
 - Upskilling and versatility: CACES and overhead crane operator training, identify technical leaders and future managers (successful Engineering training)
- Health, safety, environment
 - Reduction in significant events (reported near-misses addressed, operational permit gaps)
 - Compliance: DREAL, AFNOR
- Costs and efficiency
 - Reduced unit production costs
 - Savings generated by action plans (energy consumption, scrap, overtime hours)
 - Adherence to the department's operating budget
- Quality and continuous improvement
 - Reduced scrap rate / non-conformities
 - Decrease in customer complaints related to production
 - Numerous improvement projects launched and associated gains (Lean method, 5S, problem-solving)

## Lessons Learned

**Field presence makes all the difference**, even — and especially — when the scope expands. It is not just about setting an example in safety, but also in how to communicate with people and address everything that facilitates their work.
> Specifically: regular visits to the installation before operational meetings, commitments made and kept regarding field requests — cabins, living areas, production environment, small investments — rather than superficial presence during visits.  
> 
The manager must be clear about their level of expectation. Their long-term vision allows them to outline the trajectory and gradually engage teams, rather than imposing a rupture.
> Specifically: set the course and non-negotiables from the start, break down objectives in evaluations, coaching interviews, simply instill confidence ...
>
