# Debottleneck the repair flow to smooth out shipments

## About this project

In a steel plant, performance does not depend solely on production capacities. Finishing, repair, and cutting operations also play a decisive role in the availability of finished products and their shipment.

A complementary industrial capacity for repair and cutting constituted a bottleneck in end-of-production flows.

Its operational difficulties, combined with reliability issues and limitations on treatable product families, contributed to slowing down product availability and disrupting shipments.

A working group was set up to analyze this situation and identify improvement levers.

**Objective: improve the availability and utilization of this capacity to reduce constraints on finishing flows and secure shipments.**

## Approach

The working group structured an analysis combining industrial performance, equipment reliability, and flow organization.

The approach was organized around four axes.

**1. Measure the installation's performance**

- Analysis of operating indicators.
- Monitoring of equipment availability.
- Identification of capacity losses.
- Examination of the installation's contribution to finishing flows.
- Structuring of indicators to track evolutions.

**2. Analyze reliability losses**

A technical review highlighted a degradation in the installation's reliability and a decline in average productivity.


**3. Optimize capacity utilization**

The installation's performance was also examined from the perspective of its area of use:

- Limiting multiple passes
- Increasing pass speed
- Seeking internal and external load shedding
- Reliability improvement and organization of stops

This approach allowed evaluating the actually mobilizable capacity, beyond the equipment's nominal performance alone.

**4. Structure action tracking**

The work was integrated into a management approach combining:

- Monitoring of performance indicators.
- Improvement actions based on operators' observations
- Reliability enhancement actions.

## Contribution to the working group

I led the cross-functional Working Group, bringing together technical, operational, and organizational dimensions.

## Results

- Inventory in front of the line was divided by 4 over 6 months and subsequently stabilized.
- Monthly productivity increased by +25%.
- Reduction in unplanned stoppages and control of maintenance budgets.

## Lessons Learned

> The driver for improvement is listening to and taking into account users: it is from their observations, the difficulties they encounter, and sustained on-site presence that truly useful actions emerge.
> 

Each workshop has its importance: neglecting one fosters user demotivation.

This project illustrates the importance of a holistic approach to industrial performance, linking equipment reliability, flow organization, and the ability to make products available to customers.
