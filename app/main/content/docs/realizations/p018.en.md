# Lead technical acceptance of rotating machinery from European suppliers

## Context

In an industrial facility, the reliability of rotating machinery is essential for production continuity and mechanical risk control.

Replacing or repairing a critical asset following a failure or within a scheduled shutdown requires ensuring its technical compliance before it is made available.

This validation involves direct exchanges with suppliers, defining acceptance criteria, and analyzing the results of inspections and tests.

**Objective: Secure the acceptance of repaired or replaced rotating machinery, control technical risks, and negotiate the best maintenance and supply conditions.**

## Approach

As a Mechanical Maintenance Engineer, I carried out around ten technical acceptance interventions with European suppliers, distributed across five sites in Germany, Belgium, and the Netherlands.

My interventions were structured around four axes.

**1. Prepare technical acceptances**

- Analysis of the replacement or repair context.
- Identification of mechanical risks and critical points.
- Review of proposed maintenance procedures and operations.
- Definition of necessary acceptance inspections.
- Preparation of equipment acceptance criteria.

**2. Conduct acceptances at suppliers**

Participation in acceptance operations at supplier sites:

- Application of inspection and acceptance protocols.
- Analysis of measurements and test results.
- Verification of compliance with technical requirements.
- Identification and discussion of any deviations.
- Evaluation of equipment acceptance conditions.

**3. Negotiate technical solutions**

Exchanges with suppliers focused particularly on:

- Maintenance and repair operations.
- Technical choices and their impact on reliability.
- Risks associated with interventions.
- Availability and management of spare parts.
- Conditions necessary to secure equipment.

The objective was to find solutions adapted to the industrial constraints of the site, taking into account expected reliability and failure risks.

**4. Negotiate commercial conditions**

In addition to technical expertise, I participated in commercial negotiations with suppliers to reconcile quality requirements, cost control, and delivery conditions.

This dual approach allowed economic issues to be integrated into technical decisions.

## My Role

I managed technical acceptance interventions and associated exchanges with suppliers.

My responsibilities included:

- Preparation of rotating machinery acceptance protocols.
- Analysis of acceptance protocols and measurement results.
- Assessment of technical risks.
- Discussion and negotiation of maintenance procedures.
- Consideration of spare parts requirements.
- Technical and commercial negotiations.
- Coordination with suppliers at their industrial sites.

These missions required technical autonomy and the ability to represent the site's industrial interests in an international environment.

## Results

- **Approximately 10 technical acceptance interventions completed.**
- **5 supplier sites visited in Europe.**
- Interventions conducted in Germany, Belgium, and the Netherlands.
- Analysis of acceptance protocols and measurements on rotating machinery.
- Technical negotiations covering maintenance, mechanical risks, and spare parts.
- Commercial negotiations integrated into exchanges with suppliers.

## Lessons Learned

Accepting industrial equipment is not limited to verifying measurement results; it constitutes an essential step in controlling technical and economic risks.

**This project illustrates my ability to act autonomously with international suppliers, analyze the compliance of critical mechanical equipment, and lead technical and commercial negotiations.**
