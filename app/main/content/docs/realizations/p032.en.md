# Correcting the economic oversight of maintenance through anticipation and management

## About the Project

Industrial maintenance must balance two sometimes contradictory imperatives: ensuring facility availability and controlling the financial resources necessary for their operation.

Maintenance expenses, spare parts inventory, and investments represent significant commitments. Controlling them requires moving beyond an annual budget management logic to build a global view of needs, risks, and priorities.

In this context, I drove an initiative for **rationalizing the economic functioning of maintenance**, combining critical analysis of the situation, defining a trajectory to return to equilibrium, and strengthening operational oversight.

**The goal: regain sustainable financial balance without losing sight of reliability, safety, and industrial continuity imperatives.**

## Approach

### 1. Provide a clear diagnosis to management

I provided management with a clear and direct reading of the economic situation of maintenance.

This approach consisted in moving beyond budgetary findings to question real needs, priorities, and the mechanisms behind expenses.

I advocated for an approach based on transparency, understanding of industrial constraints, and the necessity of making decisions consistent with available resources.

**The first step of the transformation was to make the situation readable and share a realistic vision of the necessary actions.**

### 2. Build a trajectory to return to equilibrium

I structured my approach around three complementary economic levers:

- **Maintenance expenses**: examine needs, prioritize interventions, and strengthen control of commitments.
- **Spare parts**: question inventory levels and replenishment needs in light of equipment criticality.
- **Investments**: re-examine priorities, distinguish indispensable needs from deferrable operations, and prepare trade-offs.

The challenge was not to indiscriminately reduce expenses, but to allocate resources to needs with the strongest industrial justification.

This approach aimed to regain financially sustainable operation while preserving control over technical risks.

### 3. Strengthen operational management of teams

I implemented tighter oversight of maintenance activities to translate economic orientations into operational practices.

This approach relied on regular operational meetings based on simple tools: **Steering based on commitment AND expense forecasting.**

Management was a central lever: controlling expenses could not rely solely on budgetary trade-offs but had to become a shared concern in daily decisions.

### 4. Embed rationalization within a reliability logic

Financial control must not lead to systematically postponing interventions or weakening equipment.

I advocated for a trade-off logic integrating:

- The criticality of installations.
- The potential consequences of a failure.
- Needs for preventive and corrective maintenance.
- Availability of spare parts.
- Budgetary constraints and necessary investments.

The objective was to build a balance between immediate expenses, industrial risks, and long-term reliability.

## My Role

I drove this transformation initiative within the scope of my responsibilities in mechanical maintenance.

My intervention stood out through three major contributions:

**A strategic vision**

I presented a lucid diagnosis of the situation to senior management and defended a trajectory to return to financial equilibrium.

**Operational leadership**

I strengthened team management to translate economic priorities into concrete decisions and practices.

**An innovative approach to steering**

I mobilized anticipation tools to improve visibility on needs and enable earlier trade-offs.

This mission combined economic vision, knowledge of industrial risks, management, and improvement of steering methods.

## Results and Contributions

Budgetary equilibrium was reached in 6 months and exceeded to ensure annual compliance.

Rationalization of spare parts by first focusing on the most critical ones.

## Lessons Learned

**Correcting the economic situation of an industrial activity does not simply mean reducing expenses. It is necessary to make difficulties visible, define a credible trajectory, and give teams the means to implement it.**

Three convictions guided my action:

- **Lucidity precedes decision-making**: a transparent diagnosis, even when it questions established practices, enables building solid trade-offs.
- **Anticipation improves economic control**: preparing needs and commitments reduces dependence on decisions made in urgency.
- **Management transforms strategy into results**: a financial orientation becomes operational only when it is reflected in priorities, responsibilities, and the daily follow-up of teams.

**This project illustrates my ability to drive an economic turnaround vision, defend decisions with senior management, and lead managerial transformation in a demanding industrial environment.**
