# From the kit printer to 3D part manufacturing

## About the project

The project combines the assembly of a kit-delivered Ultimaker printer, followed by the design and printing of parts. It thus links physical equipment with a digital preparation chain.

### My role

Choice of the kit printer, purchase, assembly, and adjustment.
Design and printing of parts.

## Overview of scope

### Equipment

A kit-assembled Ultimaker printer.
The printer lasted about 3 years, but frequent adjustments significantly degraded print quality.
I opted for other printers: Creality Ender 3 and then Dagoma Magis.

### Design and tools

Depending on the part's complexity: FreeCAD or Tkinter.
Cura is the slicer used to prepare and manage printing

## Technical focus and approach

### From file to object

FreeCAD > STL > Cura > Printer
Several GCode file corrections were made to improve print times and fix bugs.

## Results

- Complete assembly
- Design using various tools
- Print preparation via Cura
- Maintenance and optimization of prints

## Project gallery

The added images show the Ultimaker hardware and a part on a print bed. The press is shown in its open state.

## Lessons learned

3D printing is fantastic, but it suits an experienced audience: the advertised ease degrades over time (nozzle clogging, filament breakage, cleaning, need to plan for protection against environmental effects, etc.)
The use of plastic filaments opens up an interesting field of objects. However, special filaments are delicate because they cause wear, abrasion, and contamination that require fine adjustments. A dedicated printer may be necessary.
