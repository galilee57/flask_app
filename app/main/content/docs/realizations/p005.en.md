# Pilot maintenance: redress a budget of 10 M€ and strengthen industrial reliability

## Context

Within the scope of industrial maintenance responsibilities, I managed an **annual budget of approximately 10 million euros**, covering maintenance activities for several installations.

My scope included defining the maintenance strategy, tracking spending, preparing investments, and planning industrial shutdowns, including campaign changes and annual stoppages.

Upon taking office, the financial situation was deteriorated, with a spending trajectory requiring rapid intervention.

The challenge was twofold: regain financial control without compromising the technical needs of the installations, then use this control to durably improve their reliability.

This mission required coordinating several local teams, without limiting the approach to a purely accounting one.

## Objectives

- Redress a deteriorated financial situation.
- Ensure control of a 10 M€ maintenance budget.
- Build a coherent regional strategy.
- Coordinate local teams around shared priorities.
- Prepare and prioritize investments.
- Create financial capacity to renew spare parts and improve installation reliability.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Establish a transparent diagnosis of the financial situation

Upon arrival, I observed a deteriorated spending situation.

I chose not to minimize difficulties but to make the situation readable for Management and the teams.

This transparency was the starting point for the turnaround: it was necessary to share a common understanding of constraints, risks, and financial flexibility.

The objective was not only to note discrepancies but to define a realistic trajectory to reduce them.

### 2. Implement tighter budget monitoring

I established closer management of spending to quickly detect deviations and better control commitments.

This approach relied on regular analysis of needs, spending, and priorities.

It allowed identifying necessary adjustments and limiting discrepancies before they became difficult to correct.

**From the very first accounting period, this enhanced monitoring significantly reduced budget overruns.**

### 3. Build a regional maintenance strategy

Beyond immediate rectification, I worked on defining a maintenance strategy to anticipate facility needs.

This approach combined operational constraints, maintenance requirements, technical priorities, and available financial resources.

The challenge was to shift from management primarily constrained by current expenditures to a more predictive approach.

### 4. Coordinate local teams

I provided cross-functional leadership for maintenance teams across the various sites or facilities within my scope.

My role involved building shared priorities, sharing objectives, and tracking their implementation.

This coordination required taking into account local technical realities while maintaining a regional view of the issues at stake.

The success of this leadership depended on team involvement and their understanding of the chosen trajectory.

### 5. Anticipate technical needs

I strengthened the technical analysis of maintenance needs to better forecast necessary expenditures.

This anticipation allowed distinguishing essential interventions, schedulable requirements, and actions to prioritize.

It contributed to making the budget more predictable and limiting decisions made in a hurry.

### 6. Plan industrial shutdowns

The mission also included planning shutdowns related to campaign changes and annual stoppages.

These operations represented key moments to carry out necessary interventions on equipment.

Their preparation had to integrate technical needs, production constraints, available resources, and associated costs.

Planning thus became an essential lever for cost control and maintaining industrial availability.

### 7. Prepare and track investments

I also participated in defining and tracking the investments required for the facilities.

This activity fit within an overall maintenance vision, aligning short-term needs with long-term technical evolutions.

### 8. Transform budget control into reliability improvement

After rectification in the first period, subsequent years were marked by a return to budgetary balance.

Thanks to better anticipation and more precise analysis of needs, we were able to finance the replacement of spare parts while respecting the available budget.

This evolution was particularly significant: controlling expenses was no longer just a financial objective but became a means to strengthen facility reliability.

**The budget thus shifted from an imposed constraint to a tool for technical and strategic steering.**

## My Role

**I was responsible for the maintenance budget, the associated strategy, and cross-functional management of local teams.**

## Methods and Skills

**Areas:** industrial maintenance, budget management, equipment reliability, shutdown planning, industrial investments.

**Methods:** budget tracking, variance analysis, needs anticipation, technical prioritization, planning, cross-functional management, objective-based steering.

**Skills:** industrial leadership, maintenance strategy, cost control, multi-site coordination, resource management, managerial communication, decision-making.

## Results

### Budget Correction in the First Fiscal Year

Implementing tighter tracking allowed us to **significantly reduce the budget overrun** observed when I took office.

### Sustainable Return to Balance

Subsequent fiscal years were executed with a balanced budget, thanks to better needs anticipation and more rigorous expense management.

### Spare Parts Replacement

Budget control enabled financing the replacement of necessary spare parts for facilities without exceeding the allocated envelope.

### Improved Reliability

These replacements contributed to improving equipment reliability and better preparing for their future operation.

## Lessons Learned

### 1. Transparency is Essential in Management

In a deteriorating situation, hiding difficulties does not help resolve them.

It is preferable to clearly present the facts, constraints, and risks, both to senior management and to the teams.

**Transparency creates the conditions for shared diagnosis and collective mobilization.**

### 2. A Clear Vision Gives Meaning to Efforts

To mobilize the teams, three questions must be answered:

- Where do we want to go?
- How will we get there?
- How will we verify that we are following the right path?

These questions structure the strategy and enable linking daily actions to the objectives to be achieved.

### 3. Budget management should not be purely accounting-based

A maintenance budget reflects technical needs and industrial choices.

Controlling it requires understanding the equipment, risks, and intervention priorities.

### 4. Cost control must serve industrial performance

Reducing expenses is not a success if it degrades the equipment.

The objective is to better use resources to maintain, and then improve, the reliability of installations.

**This experience illustrates my ability to combine management, technical strategy, and financial rigor: correcting a difficult budgetary situation, building a shared trajectory, and transforming cost control into a lever for improving industrial reliability.**
