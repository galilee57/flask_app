# Industrialize Process and Quality Reporting with Power BI

## About the Project

In a multi-site, multi-line industrial environment, Process and Quality teams use common indicators but work with different data sources, parameters, and business constraints.

Developing a specific Power BI report for each line multiplies similar processing steps, complicates maintenance, and limits the reuse of developments.

**Goal: design a generic, configurable, and reusable Power BI architecture** capable of adapting to the particularities of different installations.

## Developed Solution

I developed a modular architecture based on **Power Query (M language)** and an external configuration system.

The principle consists of separating five components:

1. **Data Sources**: connection to data from each installation.
2. **Business Configuration**: definition of parameters, columns, types, and limits in an Excel file.
3. **Generic Processing**: common transformations executed by reusable Power Query functions.
4. **Specific Calculations**: integration of rules specific to each production line.
5. **Output**: Power BI visualizations intended for Process and Quality analysis.

This architecture allows adapting the same reporting model to several industrial contexts without rewriting all processing steps.

## Technical Implementation

The development relies on advanced use of the M language.

**Dynamic Configuration**

- Generation of parameter lists from an Excel file.
- Dynamic renaming, typing, and selection of columns.
- Unpivoting parameters and integration of specification limits.
- Adaptation of processing steps to source characteristics.

**Modularization of Processing Steps**

Development of independent functions to manage exclusions, sampling, specific calculations, variable bounds, normalization, and data compartmentalization.

**Advanced M Programming**

Use of reusable functions, list transformations, `List.Generate`, and recursive mechanisms to meet complex processing needs.

**Deployment and Exploitation**

Publishing reports on Power BI Service, diagnosing anomalies, correcting processing steps, maintenance, and republishing.

## My Role

I was responsible for designing the generic architecture, developing Power Query processing steps, and documenting them.

I also participated in the operational exploitation of reports, from resolving data issues to making them available on Power BI Service.

Local configuration and validation of results were carried out in collaboration with business teams and plant managers.

## Results

- Development of a generic and configurable model for capability analyses.
- Sharing Power Query processing steps across different industrial contexts.
- Externalizing business parameters to facilitate report adaptation.
- Publishing and exploiting Process and Quality reports on Power BI Service.
- Building a reusable and documented technical foundation.

## Lessons Learned

> **Industrializing reporting does not mean multiplying Power BI files, but rather designing a reusable architecture.**
> 

Three principles structure this approach:

- **Separating code from configuration** to adapt processing steps without modifying their common logic.
- **Sharing without standardizing**, preserving business rules specific to each installation.
- **Designing for operations**, integrating data quality, updates, maintenance, and publishing constraints from the development phase.

The value of this approach lies in moving from a succession of independent reports to a **configurable, maintainable, and reusable reporting foundation**.
