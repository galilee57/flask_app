# Lead the resolution of strip tracking deviation defects on a production line

## About the project

An industrial production line was experiencing recurrent strip tracking deviation defects, affecting process stability and production performance.

Initial analysis distinguished two phenomena: deviations occurring during product engagement and those occurring during production.

This distinction was essential to avoid searching for a single cause for incidents that could result from different mechanisms.

**Objective: identify the causes of strip tracking deviations, coordinate corrective actions, and sustainably improve process stability.**

## Approach

As the pilot of a multidisciplinary working group, I coordinated a problem-solving approach combining technical analysis, process data, and field expertise.

The work was organized around four axes.

**1. Characterize the phenomena**

- Compilation and classification of incidents.
- Distinction between deviations at engagement and during production.
- Analysis of occurrence conditions and operating parameters.
- Identification of differences between normal and degraded situations.

**2. Identify contributing factors**

Investigations covered several dimensions:

- Design and adjustment of guiding equipment.
- Product positioning upstream.
- Engagement parameters.
- Condition and replacement frequency of critical components.
- Operating practices and differences between teams.

Cross-referencing operating data and field observations notably identified a startup practice associated with better results.

**3. Deploy corrective actions**

The working group initiated several complementary actions:

- Modification of an engagement parameter.
- Increased replacement frequency of a critical consumable.
- Generalization and standardization of an operating practice.
- Adjustment of a centering system.
- Modification of a guiding component.

These interventions enabled action on both process parameters, operational practices, and equipment.

**4. Consolidate improvements**

Initial results were tracked to evaluate the effectiveness of actions and identify phenomena still requiring control.

The approach was extended by a more significant technical evolution of the guidance system, installed subsequently to reinforce process stability.

## My Role

I led the working group, coordinating contributions from all business units involved in resolving the issue.

My responsibilities included:

- Structuring the analysis approach.
- Coordinating process, mechanical, and operational investigations.
- Exploiting operational data and field feedback.
- Identifying and prioritizing improvement paths.
- Tracking corrective actions and their results.
- Capitalizing on and standardizing practices.

Defining and implementing solutions mobilized complementary skills from production, maintenance, and technical specialist teams.

## Results

- Identification of two distinct mechanisms behind strip tracking deviation defects.
- Implementation of corrective actions on parameters, equipment, and operating practices.
- Reduction of certain incidents following initial adjustments and operational changes.
- Standardization of a more favorable startup practice.
- Subsequent installation of an evolution to the guidance system.
- Sustainable improvement in line behavior, confirmed through lessons learned.

## Lessons Learned

**Sustainably resolving an industrial issue requires distinguishing failure mechanisms, mobilizing appropriate expertise, and combining multiple levels of action.**

In this case, a notable difference in success emerged between one team and the others. By discussing directly with operators, I observed that this difference stemmed from a much better-controlled engagement protocol. It was generalized to other teams and directly locked into the line automation.
