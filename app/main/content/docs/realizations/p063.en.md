# Define and pilot a Big Data strategy to transform Industrial Quality

## Context

In a complex industrial environment, Quality teams possess a considerable data heritage: process measurements, production history, product characteristics, inspection results, inspection images, industrial events, and technical documents.

However, this information is distributed across numerous systems, with different structures, acquisition frequencies, and quality levels.

Exploiting this data often requires manual extractions, complex reconciliations, and specific treatments for each study.

This organization limits the ability to capitalize on analyses and reuse data for new needs.

**I contributed to defining and piloting, from a business perspective, a Big Data approach aimed at transforming this data heritage into a sustainable analysis capability serving Industrial Quality.**

The ambition went beyond conducting a few Data Science studies: it was about evolving working methods, tools, and skills to enable more systematic data exploitation.

## Objectives

The program pursued several complementary objectives:

- Facilitate access to industrial data necessary for Quality analyses.
- Reduce dependence on manual extractions and preparations.
- Structure a portfolio of use cases addressing concrete industrial problems.
- Develop user autonomy in data exploration.
- Align business needs with the capabilities of Data and IT teams.
- Develop a data-driven decision-making culture.

## Approach

### 1. Build a value-oriented business vision

The first dimension of the program consisted in positioning Big Data as a lever for improving industrial performance, rather than as a technological end in itself.


### 2. Structure data access needs

One of the major challenges concerned data availability.

Industrial studies frequently require combining multiple sources: process parameters, production events, product characteristics, Quality controls, and temporal histories.

I worked on identifying business needs and formalizing them with users.

These needs were then matched against technical possibilities with IT and Data teams.

The objective was to promote the progressive availability of data in a shared environment.

### 3. Lead a portfolio of Quality use cases

The program relied on industrial use cases to concretely demonstrate the value of data operations.

The approach drew inspiration from structured methods for solving industrial problems, notably A3 logic.

**The program's role was to bring forth, structure, and support these use cases, while fostering the capitalization of methods and results.**

### 4. Develop an open approach to analytical tools

Not all users possess the same technical skills, and not all problems require the same tools.

The strategy therefore had to enable multiple levels of data operations.

- Tools accessible to business users: visualization solutions and no-code environments.
- Programming environments: Python, statistical and Machine Learning libraries, as well as PySpark
- Big Data environments: Data Lake and distributed processing

This diversity of tools was based on a conviction: **technological choice must depend on the problem to be solved, the volume of data, and the level of autonomy sought for users.**

### 5. Support the upskilling of teams

Making data available is not enough to transform practices.

The program therefore included a significant dimension of user support.

I contributed to promoting available tools, sharing analysis methods, and facilitating access to training resources.

This approach also aimed to evolve historical practices that relied mainly on office-based extractions and manual processing.

### 6. Evolve studies towards reproducible processing

Another important dimension of the program concerned the transition from exploratory analysis to more structured technical processing.

I notably contributed to formalizing an automation use case in an Azure DevOps repository.

### 7. Contribute to program governance and monitoring

The transformation required continuous coordination between business units, IT teams, and Data specialists.

I contributed to project monitoring, the identification of priority needs, and exchanges allowing their feasibility to be evaluated.

## My Role

**I held business leadership responsibilities and contributed to building the Big Data vision for Quality activities.**

This positioning allowed me to combine a cross-functional view of industrial challenges with direct technical involvement in certain tasks.

I was not only a requester of Data solutions: I could also contribute to their design, experimentation, and formalization.

## Results

**The progressive implementation of a structured Big Data approach centered on Quality needs, available data, use cases, and the upskilling of users.**

- The formalization of an industrial data operations vision.
- The structuring of ingestion needs and data availability requirements.
- The organization of a portfolio of use cases.
- The use of analysis methods adapted to Quality issues.
- The dissemination of resources and practices fostering user autonomy.
- The contribution to Data project governance.
- The creation of a documented Azure DevOps repository to prepare for the automation of a Quality process.
- The evolution of certain studies into more easily reusable technical assets.

The program thus contributed to moving beyond a logic based exclusively on ad-hoc analyses to build a more structured and cross-functional approach.

## Lessons Learned

**Build reusable capacity.** Data preparation should not need to be systematically redone for every study.

**Develop skills alongside technologies.** User autonomy is an essential factor for success.

**Do not oppose no-code and programming.** Both approaches are complementary and address different needs.

**Measure value up to usage.** The true culmination of a Data project is its regular use and its demonstrated impact on operations.

**This program illustrates my ability to drive a Data transformation vision, manage complex business needs, and concretely contribute to their translation into technical solutions, combining industrial expertise, Big Data, Data Science, and change management support.**
