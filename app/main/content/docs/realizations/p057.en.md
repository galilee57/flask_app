# Digitize the management and traceability of sleeves on an industrial line

## About the Project

On a repair industrial line, sleeves are essential equipment for mandrel configuration and winding operations.

Depending on product dimensions and characteristics, different types and grades of sleeves must be used.

Their management relies on several operational constraints: availability, location, compatibility with products, wear status, and organization of assembly and disassembly operations.

A primarily manual management presents several limitations:

- Difficulty in precisely knowing the location of each sleeve.
- Risk of error in equipment selection or assembly.
- Lack of visibility on usage history.
- Difficulty in correlating audit results with actual sleeve exposure during production.
- Complex management of new equipment intake and scrapping.
- Time spent searching for, moving, and preparing sleeves before interventions.

**The challenge was to transform this manual management into a reliable computerized system capable of supporting daily operations and improving control over the equipment fleet.**

## Objectives

The project aimed to design structured sleeve management around six objectives:

1. Ensure individual traceability and location of each sleeve.
2. Secure assemblies by considering configurations and products.
3. Monitor equipment status based on audits and usage history.
4. Manage the lifecycle of sleeves, from introduction to scrapping.
5. Facilitate preparation and supply of equipment at the overhead crane.
6. Reduce time losses related to assembly and disassembly operations.

## Approach

### 1. Structure sleeve identification and location

The first step was to have reliable knowledge of the fleet.

Each sleeve had to be individually identified and associated with its essential information (Reference and technical specifications, type and grade of sleeve, physical location, movement history, etc.)

This traceability formed the foundation of the future computerized system.

### 2. Secure assembly configurations

Sleeve characteristics must be adapted to manufactured products and mandrel configurations.

This approach aimed to facilitate the selection of appropriate parts and reduce risks of error during configuration changes.

**The objective was to move from a selection largely based on operators' knowledge to a management approach supported by structured information and compatibility rules.**

### 3. Integrate audits and usage history

The condition of the sleeves directly influences the quality of the winding.

A degraded sleeve can cause defects on the products, particularly when the strip comes into contact with damaged areas.

The project aimed to link findings from audits with the usage history of each piece of equipment.

This organization was intended to improve knowledge of sleeve wear and provide objective elements to guide inspections and replacement decisions.

### 4. Manage equipment lifecycle

The system was also intended to cover fleet evolutions.

Integrating a new sleeve required its identification, recording of its characteristics, and assignment to a location.

Conversely, scrapping allowed removing an equipment from the available fleet while preserving its history.

This lifecycle management aimed to maintain a reliable inventory and prevent an unsuitable or retired-from-service equipment from being selected for assembly.

### 5. Optimize assembly preparation and overhead crane movements

Configuration changes require handling and movement operations of the sleeves.

Preparing equipment for the overhead crane therefore represented a lever for improving productivity.

The project aimed to reduce assembly and disassembly times.

**Digitalization was thus intended to directly contribute to better preparation of interventions and to reducing line downtime.**

### 6. Integrate monitoring into operator supervision

Sleeve management concerns several functions: production, maintenance, quality, and internal logistics.

The computerized system was intended to constitute a common reference for information related to equipment.

## My Role

I contributed to the transformation approach of sleeve management, leveraging my knowledge of line operation and industrial constraints.

This approach illustrates my ability to translate operational constraints into functional requirements for a computerized system.

## Results

Commissioning and integration of monitoring into supervision by the IT teams.  
During the project, remote management of lubrication was also integrated into the suite.

## Lessons Learned

>Digitalizing industrial equipment does not merely consist of replacing a paper file or register with an application. It requires structuring all the rules governing its use.
> 

**Digital transformation must start from the shop floor.** An effective tool must integrate the real constraints faced by operators, maintenance teams, and production.

**This project illustrates my ability to analyze an industrial process, structure complex management rules, and design a digitalization approach focused on reliability, quality, and productivity.**
