# Identify the causes of surface defects and ensure industrial equipment reliability

## Context

A steel production line encountered periodic surface defects, including marks such as "facets" and "embossments", affecting product quality.

Several incidents appeared to be associated with a particular roll. The frequency of replacement of this equipment increased, and some roll changes led to the disappearance of defects.

However, the responsible mechanisms remained uncertain, and roll traceability did not always allow for precise reconstruction of their repair and usage history.

**Objective: identify the mechanisms causing defects, understand the role of equipment, and implement actions to prevent their recurrence.**

## Approach

I contributed to a structured technical investigation, combining defect analysis, historical data, field observations, and mechanical expertise.

The approach was organized around four axes.

**1. Characterize defects and search for their origin**

- Analysis of the periodicity of marks on products.
- Correlation between defect pitch and geometric characteristics of rolls.
- Identification of a specific roll as suspect equipment.
- Construction of a cause tree to explore possible mechanisms.

**2. Reconstruct equipment history**

Collection and correlation of information regarding rolls:

- Installation and replacement dates.
- Rectification and repair operations.
- Diameters and geometric characteristics.
- Surface conditions and roughness.
- Surface treatments and interventions by service providers.

This work allowed for the structuring of scattered information and examination of relationships between roll characteristics and periods of defect occurrence.

**3. Confront technical hypotheses**

Several mechanisms were examined:

- **Product origin**: search for a pre-existing defect on the strip.
- **Vibrations**: observation of strip behavior and vibration checks.
- **Roll condition**: analysis of geometry, roughness, and surface characteristics.
- **Equipment repair**: comparison of rectification and surface treatment practices through visits to suppliers.

The investigations allowed certain leads to be ruled out and focused the analysis on roll characteristics and their repair conditions, without considering all hypotheses as definitively proven.

**4. Reliability of equipment and practices**

The work was extended by several control actions:

- Individual identification of rolls.
- Strengthening traceability of repair operations.
- Evolution of plans and technical files.
- Integration of identifiers into expertise files.
- Improvement of equipment monitoring and their history tracking.

These actions aim to facilitate future investigations and limit the recurrence of defects.

## A key step: distinguishing defect mechanisms

The investigations also led to questioning the initial classification of defects.

Under the generic term "facets", several visually similar but physically different phenomena were grouped together.

The analysis notably allowed distinguishing:

- Defects associated with substrate deformation.
- Defects linked to transfer or surface marking of the coating.

This distinction enabled better orientation of investigations and adaptation of detection means and prevention actions to the mechanisms actually studied.

## My role

I contributed directly to structuring and advancing the technical investigation.

My interventions covered:

- Collection and consolidation of equipment histories.
- Analysis of relationships between quality incidents and roll characteristics.
- Formulation and documentation of technical hypotheses.
- Contribution to cause trees and problem-solving analyses.
- Synthesis of investigations and follow-up of actions.
- Monitoring of reliability measures for roll identification and traceability.

These works were carried out with the Process, Quality, Maintenance, and Operations teams, as well as with the relevant technical specialists.

## Results

- Identification of a suspect equipment based on defect periodicity.
- Reconstruction and structuring of roll technical histories.
- Clarification of several defect mechanisms previously grouped under a single term.
- Orientation of investigations toward roll characteristics and their repair conditions.
- Strengthening of individual identification and traceability of equipment.
- Evolution of technical documents and monitoring practices to facilitate prevention and future analyses.

## Lessons learned

Effective root cause analysis relies as much on physical understanding of the defect as on the quality of available information about equipment.

Industrial reliability does not rely solely on correcting an equipment, but also on **the ability to understand, document, and control its behavior over time**.
