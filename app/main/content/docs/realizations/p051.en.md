# Develop a quality diagnostic system based on business rules and Power BI

## About the Project

Width out-of-tolerances constituted a recurring quality issue on an industrial production line.

Each new episode could require restarting a complete approach: data collection, analysis of manufacturing parameters, root cause search, and definition of corrective actions.

However, a significant part of the necessary knowledge already existed in previous analyses, FMEAs (AMDEC), and technical group work.

**The challenge was to transform this dispersed expertise into a reproducible analysis system capable of automatically exploiting production data.**

The ambition went beyond simple defect visualization: it involved building a first level of diagnosis and strengthening the monitoring of industrial deviations.

## Objectives

I developed an approach combining process data, expert rules, and Power BI to:

- Automate part of the first-level analyses.
- Identify potential causes of width out-of-tolerances.
- Leverage knowledge from previous investigations.
- Observe the temporal evolution of defects and suspected causes.
- Detect trends and early signs of deviation.
- Facilitate prioritization of investigations and corrective actions.

## Approach

### 1. Leverage Industrial Knowledge

We revisited historical analyses, FMEAs (AMDEC), technical group work, and hypotheses formulated during previous investigations.

A structured analysis using the **6M / Ishikawa** method allowed organizing potential cause families.

For each one, we searched for industrial parameters likely to provide diagnostic elements.

The goal was to move from knowledge essentially held by experts to structured, explicit, and reusable knowledge.

### 2. Build a Process Data Model

I built the Power BI tool by integrating data from the production line and business information necessary for analyzing the phenomenon.

Combining these sources allowed associating dimensional defects with corresponding manufacturing conditions.

This step was essential to establish a common analytical basis and reduce the manual manipulations required for each investigation.

### 3. Translate domain knowledge into diagnostic rules

The core of the system relied on transforming technical hypotheses into **conditional rules applied to process data**.

Each rule analyzed one or more parameters and suggested an investigation path when certain conditions were met.

For example, based on thresholds and criteria defined by experts:

- **Temperature exceeding a threshold**: signal verification of the furnace model.
- **Tension below a reference value**: suggest inspection of the reduction table.
- **Significant deviation on a process parameter**: direct analysis toward the corresponding family of causes.

These examples illustrate the system's principle: rules do not automatically demonstrate the actual cause of a defect but provide an initial technical direction based on available data.

**Expert knowledge thus became a set of explicit rules, applicable in a homogeneous manner to each new production run.**

### 4. Automate first-level analysis in Power BI

The rules were integrated into the Power BI tool to produce repeatable analyses without manually reconstructing each diagnosis.

The system enabled bringing together:

- Products exhibiting width defects.
- Their manufacturing conditions.
- Triggered business rules.
- Proposed potential causes.
- The evolution of these signals over time.

This automation facilitated the identification of situations requiring deeper investigation.

It also contributed to reducing dependence on ad-hoc analyses and manual extractions.

### 5. Detect trends and early deviations

The system's interest did not stop at the individual diagnosis of a product.

By retaining rule results over time, it became possible to analyze the frequency of signal occurrence and their evolution.

**The system thus enabled moving from post-defect analysis to regular monitoring of conditions likely to cause it.**

### 6. Embed the tool in a continuous improvement loop

Power BI was published and integrated into a recurring business review process.

Results could be compared with field observations to:

- Verify the relevance of proposed causes.
- Identify situations not covered by existing rules.
- Adjust thresholds and diagnostic conditions.
- Prioritize investigations.
- Capitalize on lessons from new incidents.

The rule model was therefore designed as an evolving system, progressively enriched by industrial experience.

A complementary exploration of clustering and Machine Learning methods was also undertaken to study possibilities beyond expert rules.

## My Role

I designed and developed this data-driven quality analysis approach based on industrial data and business rules.

This work was part of a collaboration with process, quality, and process expert teams.

## Results

**A quality diagnostic support system published and deployed within a recurring monitoring framework.**

Drifts are now detected earlier, enabling teams to delve deeper with the relevant services.

The scrap rate was halved.

## Lessons Learned

>When discussing Artificial Intelligence, people primarily refer to Generative AI. However, a rule-based system—which capitalizes on and makes available domain expertise—is often a more effective AI building block. It is stable; it relies on predictable causes; it is resource-efficient; and it does not require permanent access to agents or LLMs.
> 

**This project illustrates my ability to transform industrial expertise into automated diagnostic logic, leverage process data with Power BI, and develop continuous improvement-oriented decision support tools.**
