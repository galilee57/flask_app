# Deploy vibration monitoring for 5 critical machines to anticipate failures

## About the Project

In an industrial installation, the reliability of rotating machinery is a major issue for production continuity, equipment safety, and maintenance cost control.

Some critical machines pose significant risks in case of mechanical failure: bearing deterioration, degradation of rotating components, unplanned shutdowns, or damage requiring heavy repairs.

Monitoring based solely on periodic inspections does not always allow for early enough identification of changes in their behavior.

**Objective: Implement continuous monitoring of five critical rotating machines to detect signs of degradation, anticipate interventions, and ensure reliable operation.**

## Solution

I proposed and supported the deployment of an online **Bently Nevada** monitoring system on five critical rotating machines at the site.

The device enables continuous tracking of parameters representative of their mechanical behavior, notably vibrations, to detect evolutions that may reveal anomalies.

Monitoring provides maintenance teams with complementary information to traditional inspections for:

- Detecting changes in vibratory behavior.
- Identifying signs of mechanical degradation.
- Monitoring the evolution of bearing condition.
- Evaluating the need and urgency of an intervention.
- Preparing shutdowns under conditions compatible with process constraints.
- Improving equipment availability and reliability.

This approach evolves maintenance from a mainly periodic or corrective logic to **condition-based maintenance based on the actual state of the machines**.

## Approach

**1. Identify the need and propose the investment**

As a Mechanical Maintenance Engineer at the site, I identified the interest of permanent monitoring of the most critical rotating equipment.

I proposed investing in a monitoring solution to strengthen the diagnostic and anticipation capabilities of the maintenance teams.

**2. Pilot implementation**

I followed the deployment of the Bently Nevada system on the five concerned machines.

This step included coordinating the setup of the device and its integration into equipment monitoring practices.

**3. Support maintenance teams**

The value of a monitoring system depends on its proper operation.

I supported the training of maintenance teams in using the device and interpreting the provided information.

The objective was to enable teams to detect abnormal evolutions and integrate them into their maintenance decisions.

**4. Exploit data to guide interventions**

Vibration monitoring allows tracking operating trends and identifying signs of impending degradation.

This information contributes to determining the appropriate time for intervention, taking into account both the mechanical condition of the machine and production constraints.

## My Role

I ensured **technical support for the project**, from proposing the investment to its adoption by maintenance teams.

My responsibilities included:

- Identifying the need for monitoring critical machines.
- Proposing and justifying the investment.
- Tracking the implementation of the Bently Nevada system.
- Coordinating its deployment across five machines.
- Supporting and training maintenance teams.
- Integrating monitoring into diagnostic and maintenance practices.

This project combines expertise in industrial mechanics, vibration analysis, investment management, and operational change leadership.

## Results

- **Five critical rotating machines equipped with a Bently Nevada monitoring system.**
- Implementation of continuous monitoring of their mechanical behavior.
- Development of diagnostic and anticipation capabilities for maintenance teams.
- Integration of vibration data into equipment condition tracking.
- Identification and technical justification of a defect thanks to recorded information.
- **Handling of a long production shutdown by the site's insurance, allowing investment payback from its first use.**

## Lessons Learned

**Continuous monitoring of critical machines enables transforming physical signals into actionable information for maintenance and industrial risk control.**

Three principles emerge from this experience:

- **Anticipate rather than react**: early detection of mechanical evolutions allows preparing interventions before a failure causes an uncontrolled shutdown.
- **Combine technology and domain expertise**: a monitoring system adds value only if teams know how to interpret data and translate it into operational decisions.
- **Leverage data beyond maintenance**: recording operating parameters can also provide objective elements to characterize an incident and justify financial coverage.

**This project demonstrates my ability to lead an industrial reliability investment, deploy a monitoring technology, and achieve concrete technical and economic benefits.**
