# Lead the migration and multi-site deployment of a Statistical Process Control (SPC) platform

## About the Project

In a multi-site industrial environment, Statistical Process Control (SPC — Statistical Process Control) is an essential tool for monitoring process stability, detecting deviations, and supporting continuous improvement initiatives.

The company had a legacy control chart management solution that gradually faced maintainability and compatibility issues with modern IT environments: a modernization program was launched to migrate to a new fully web-based platform.

This transformation aimed to preserve the essential functionalities of the legacy tool while enabling new use cases: Process and Maintenance dashboards, statistical indicators, data source management, and rights administration.

The project presented a particular challenge: **the technical migration had to be accompanied by validation of industrial use cases and adoption of the new solution by teams across multiple sites.**

I led the **business management of this program**, in coordination with IT teams, and took charge of supporting deployment on the sites as well as training Key Users.

## Objectives

The project pursued several objectives:

- Replace a legacy application that had become difficult to maintain.
- Ensure continuity of statistical process monitoring functions.
- Validate the new solution on real industrial use cases.
- Evolve functionalities based on user needs.
- Coordinate business teams, IT teams, and developers.
- Organize progressive deployment across multiple sites.
- Support migration of control charts and existing use cases.
- Train Key Users to develop local team autonomy.
- Promote sustainable adoption of the new platform.

## Approach

### 1. Lead business requirement expression

My role was to represent industrial needs in a project with a strong IT component.

It was necessary to ensure that the future platform would meet the expectations of different users:

- Operators responsible for monitoring control charts.
- Process and Quality Managers.
- Chart managers and administrators.
- Maintenance teams.
- Heads of various industrial sites.

This approach required distinguishing essential functions for business continuity, desired improvements, and technical constraints to be taken into account.

**The challenge was to translate industrial use cases into functional requirements understandable and exploitable by IT teams.**

### 2. Organize and evaluate an industrial Proof of Concept

A POC phase was conducted on a pilot scope to evaluate the new platform before generalization.

The experimentation notably covered a dozen control charts representative of industrial usage scenarios.

I developed a functional evaluation framework structuring the tests, observations, and recommendations.

The tests covered several dimensions:

- Creation and configuration of control charts.
- Use of various chart types.
- Data and source management.
- Creation of calculated columns.
- Visualization and recalculation of data.
- Exploitation of capability indicators, notably Pp and Ppk.
- Ergonomics and navigation.
- Reporting features.
- Dashboard construction.

The objective was not only to verify that the application functioned, but to determine whether it could meet the actual practices of users.

### 3. Transforming field feedback into functional enhancements

The POC enabled the identification of limitations and complementary needs.

Some concerned temporal data aggregation, others dashboard ergonomics, reporting functions, or interactions with the industrial information system.

I structured the evaluation results and formulated recommendations for the development teams.

This approach facilitated exchanges between business users and technical teams.

**The POC thus became a genuine functional steering tool: field feedback was transformed into concrete enhancement requests.**

### 4. Coordinating deployment with IT teams

After the evaluation phase, the project entered a progressive deployment process.

I ensured business leadership in coordination with IT teams, who were responsible for technical aspects of migration and integration.

Coordination covered several topics:

- Preparation of sites for platform changeover.
- Identification of usage patterns and charts to be retained.
- Monitoring of functional validations.
- Reporting and handling of anomalies.
- Management of gaps between business needs and available features.
- Supporting users during version changes.
- Addressing constraints related to rights, data, and architecture.

Particular attention was paid to the difference between a completed IT migration and a solution actually usable in the field.

**Deployment could only be considered successful when users regained their essential functions and could work autonomously.**

### 5. Supporting industrial sites

Multi-site deployment required accounting for local practices that sometimes differed.

Sites did not necessarily have the same control charts, data sources, or levels of maturity in SPC tool usage.

I supported local teams in preparing for and adopting the new solution.

This approach included presenting features, identifying difficulties, sharing best practices, and tracking progress.

It also enabled the reporting of specific needs and capitalizing on lessons learned from one site for the benefit of others.

### 6. Train Key Users and foster local autonomy

I took charge of training the Key Users, who are essential liaisons for deployment across various sites.

These reference users needed to be able to understand the new platform, master its main features, and support local teams.

The training covered tool usage, including consultation and management of maps, analysis functions, dashboards, and best practices for use.

Beyond knowledge transfer, the objective was to build a network of users capable of contributing to solution adoption and facilitating the resolution of common issues.

**This approach ensured that each site would not remain permanently dependent on the central project team.**

## My Role

**I led the business-side management of the project, interfacing with IT teams, and supported the deployment of the new platform across industrial sites.**

This role required combining statistical expertise, knowledge of industrial processes, project management, and change management support.

## Results

**A migration validated at 97% and then deployed across multiple industrial sites, with structured business-side management and a user support mechanism.**

## Lessons Learned

**The success of an industrial digitalization project is not measured solely by the go-live of software, but by its ability to be sustainably used by teams.**

**Key Users are essential actors in change.** Their training enables the development of local autonomy and facilitates adoption.

**Multi-site operations require balancing standardization with local specificities.** One must seek common practices without ignoring the constraints specific to each installation.

**This project illustrates my ability to lead the business dimension of a multi-site industrial software program, collaborate with IT teams, and drive change down to the end users.**
