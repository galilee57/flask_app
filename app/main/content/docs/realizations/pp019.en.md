# Writing for a local newspaper, from angle to final proof approval

## About the project

For six years, I wrote articles for a local newspaper. Within the editorial committee, I participated in topic selection and defining their angle. For the articles I took charge of, I met with the people concerned, conducted interviews and photos, then tracked the preparation of the final proof approval (BAT) with the publishing house journalist.

## Approach

**Define topics and their angle in the editorial committee.** We decided together on the topics to cover and how to approach them. The angle gave direction to the article and guided the questions to ask in the field.

**Meet, interview, and photograph.** I collected testimonials from the people concerned. These meetings allowed me to refine the topic based on their experience, and I took the photos intended to accompany the articles.

**Write and track final proof approval.** I structured the information and testimonials collected to create a clear article accessible to readers. I then exchanged with the publishing house journalist during the preparation of the final proof approval (BAT), the last step before printing.

## My Role

I intervened at two levels. I participated in the collective decisions of the editorial committee and took charge of producing my articles, from preparing interviews to editorial follow-up before printing.

## Results

I contributed over six years to the publication of articles combining texts, testimonials, and photographs. This experience allowed me to work on the entire editorial process, from a topic discussed in the committee to its preparation for publication with the publishing house.

## Lessons Learned

A clear angle helps ask better questions and choose what deserves to be told. Interviews can also evolve this angle when they reveal an aspect of the topic that the committee had not considered. I learned to balance these two requirements in writing, building a narrative accessible to readers while remaining faithful to the collected statements.
