# YouTube to Obsidian — From Idea to App Store Publication with Codex

## Context

I use Obsidian to organize my knowledge and keep resources I want to revisit. A YouTube video can be part of these resources, for example when it sheds light on a subject I am studying or feeds an ongoing project. I wanted to simplify the process between the moment I spot a video and the moment I integrate it into my notes.

I made this the starting point of **YouTube to Obsidian**, my first iOS app. I pursued two related objectives. The first was to meet a need for my own use. The second was to learn how to lead a mobile app project to its publication on the App Store, relying on Codex for development.

## Approach

**Define a precise usage:** I centered the application on an identifiable action, integrating a YouTube video into my Obsidian notes space. This scope gave me a criterion to orient the project. Development choices had to serve this path and remain consistent with my way of using Obsidian.

**Develop the app with Codex:** I started from the path I wanted to obtain to guide the development work. Describing to Codex the action to be performed and the expected result led me to clarify elements that seemed obvious to me as a user. This precision allowed me to relate development proposals to the initial usage.

**Lead the project to publication:** I continued the work until making the application available on the App Store. For this first iOS project, this step was part of the learning. It allowed me to discover distribution requirements and reach a version accessible to other users.

## My Role

I identified the need, defined the scope of the application, and led the project to its publication. I relied on **Codex as a development assistant**, while retaining responsibility for product choices and the published version.

This experience gave me two roles on the same project. As a user, I could formulate the problem based on a concrete situation. As the project owner, I had to transform this expectation into a precise enough path to guide development and bring it to completion.

## Result

**YouTube to Obsidian is my first app published on the App Store.** The project resulted in an application available publicly and gave me my first experience of the iOS product realization cycle, from need definition to distribution.

## Lessons Learned

**Concrete usage helps decide.** Starting from a situation I knew, I could evaluate a proposal by returning to a simple question. Does it facilitate moving from a YouTube video to my Obsidian notes?

**Developing with Codex requires making intent explicit.** A request is easier to guide and verify when I can describe the user's action and the result I expect. This way of working links technical exchanges to a product decision.

**Publishing is part of the realization work.** Leading this first project to the App Store allowed me to discover iOS distribution by applying it to an application I had designed for a real need.
