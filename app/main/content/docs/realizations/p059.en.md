# Develop a quality diagnostic model based on business rules in Excel

## Context

On a continuous production industrial line, certain surface defects exhibited complex occurrence mechanisms.

Their analysis required cross-referencing quality observations, manufacturing parameters, and process knowledge to identify conditions favoring their occurrence.

In this context, different modeling approaches were considered, including Machine Learning methods aimed at finding relationships between industrial parameters and observed defects.

However, implementing a sophisticated predictive system raised several challenges: data availability, defect definition, model reliability, result interpretation, and solution maintenance.

**I chose to develop a complementary approach based on explicit business rules, directly implemented in an Excel spreadsheet.**

The objective was to have a usable model without creating an IT infrastructure disproportionate to the need.

## Objectives

I wanted to build a tool capable of:

- Formalizing process knowledge in the form of understandable rules.
- Enabling rapid evolution of rules.
- Limiting costs and maintenance constraints.
- Making a directly usable model available

## Approach

### 1. Understand physical mechanisms

The first step consisted of analyzing the industrial phenomena at the origin of the studied defects.

Unlike an approach relying solely on machine learning, building a rule-based model requires making explicit the known or assumed relationships between process parameters and risky situations.

This approach directly leverages domain expertise.

### 2. Translate process expertise into rules

I developed a deterministic model based on conditional rules.

The principle consists of translating process knowledge into exploitable logical conditions.

For example, a rule can take the form of:

**IF an operating condition is met AND a parameter exceeds a threshold, THEN identify a situation requiring special attention.**

Rules can also be combined to represent more complex situations.

This formalization offers a significant advantage: each result can be linked to the conditions that produced it.

The model therefore remains explainable and directly comparable to industrial knowledge.

### 3. Implementing the Model in Excel

I developed the model directly in an Excel spreadsheet: This choice was intentional.

Excel allows structuring parameters, formalizing rules, performing calculations, and presenting results without requiring the development of a specific application.

**The model fit within a simple spreadsheet while meeting the need for formalization and exploitation of business rules.**

This solution allowed focusing efforts on the relevance of the rules and their usage, rather than on building and maintaining a complex technical chain.

## My Role

**I personally designed and developed the rule-based model in Excel.**

This work illustrates my ability to simultaneously leverage my knowledge of industrial processes, analytical skills, and technical judgment in selecting a solution.

## Results

**A diagnostic model based on business rules, developed in a simple Excel spreadsheet.**

Its use as a prerequisite for operational meetings to precisely target the departments to engage (verify regulation, setpoint temperature, sensor calibration, etc.)

## Lessons Learned

>A simple and well-designed model can deliver more operational value than a sophisticated system that is difficult to understand, exploit, or maintain.
> 

This experience reinforced several convictions.

**Complexity is not an end in itself.** The choice of technology must be justified by the problem to be solved, not by its level of sophistication.

**Domain expertise is a modeling resource.** Knowledge of physical mechanisms can often be directly translated into explicit rules.

**Maintainability is part of performance.** A solution that remains easy to adapt over time holds significant operational advantage.

**A spreadsheet can constitute a genuine modeling tool.** When the scope and data volume allow, Excel can suffice to build a relevant business model.

This project illustrates my ability to design a pragmatic analytical solution, translate industrial expertise into operational rules, and prioritize efficiency and sustainability over technological complexity.
