# Automate surface defect classification to analyze production campaigns

## Context

Surface inspection systems generate a large volume of defect images during production campaigns.

While these images constitute a valuable source of information on product quality, their exploitation remains limited when defect categories are too general or insufficiently representative of observed phenomena.

Campaign analysis nevertheless requires the ability to identify, quantify, and locate homogeneous families of defects in order to relate their occurrence to manufacturing conditions.

**Objective: Develop an automatic image classification method to enrich campaign analyses with more precise quality indicators.**

## Approach

I experimented with a Machine Learning chain combining **Orange Data Mining and Python**, from image preparation to reusing a trained model on new populations.

The approach relies on four steps.

**1. Build a learning population**

- Selection and organization of defect images.
- Constitution of previously identified and labeled categories.
- Data preparation for learning.
- Transformation of images into digital representations exploitable by algorithms.

**2. Train and compare models**

Use Orange workflows to experiment with different classification algorithms.

Evaluation relies in particular on confusion matrices to identify correctly recognized categories and classification errors.

Model comparison allows selecting an approach adapted to the studied defect families.

**3. Automate recognition of new images**

Once the model is selected:

- Save the trained model.
- Load into an independent classification workflow.
- Predict categories for new images.
- Visual control of results.
- Optional enrichment of learning data and retraining.

This separation between learning and prediction allows reusing a classifier without systematically reconstructing the model.

**4. Prepare operations in campaign analyses**

The objective is to associate predicted categories with production information to obtain a more detailed view of the defects encountered during campaigns.

This approach opens the possibility of tracking the distribution of defect families, their frequency and location, then comparing these observations with process data.

## Technical Achievements

**Machine Learning with Orange**

Construction of graphical workflows integrating image preparation, feature extraction, learning, evaluation and prediction.

**Performance Evaluation**

Comparison of algorithms and analysis of confusion matrices to understand the performance and recognition limits of different categories.

**Deep Learning and Convolutional Networks**

Study and documentation of the principles of convolutional neural networks (CNN), including convolution, pooling and classification operations.

**Python and Extensibility**

Use of Python as a complement to low-code workflows and exploration of possibilities for evolution towards more advanced modelling environments.

**Model Reuse**

Implementation of an experimental approach separating model construction, evaluation and backup from its use for classifying new images.

## My Role

I contributed to the experimentation and structuring of this image classification approach, by associating Machine Learning tools with industrial quality analysis needs.

My work focused on:

- Preparation of image populations.
- Construction of classification workflows with Orange.
- Experimentation and comparison of models.
- Analysis of performance and recognition errors.
- Backup and reuse of models.
- Documentation and sharing of Machine Learning and Deep Learning methods.

## Results

- Construction of a complete experimental chain for image classification.
- Comparative evaluation of Machine Learning models.
- Use of confusion matrices to analyse classification errors.
- Backup and reuse of a model to predict categories of new images.
- Definition of an approach enabling the exploitation of classifications in production campaign analyses.

## Lessons Learned

> The industrial interest of a classification model does not reside in its ability to recognize an image, but in producing usable information at the scale of production.
>
