# Prepare and manage industrial maintenance shutdowns using a structured repository in Maximo

## Context

As the regional maintenance manager, I oversaw the preparation and management of maintenance shutdowns across several industrial units, representing budgets of **€1 to €3 million per shutdown**.

The challenge was to carry out necessary interventions within a constrained budgetary framework without compromising service quality or personnel safety.

Preparation included defining the work scope, conducting consultations, negotiating contracts and services, financial management, and site organization.

I chose to go beyond a preparation logic limited to the upcoming shutdown by initiating in-depth work on describing maintenance procedures in the Maximo CMMS.

**The goal was to transform one-off preparation efforts into a lasting investment for future shutdowns.**

## Objectives

- Prepare and manage industrial shutdowns within budget constraints.
- Ensure service quality and equipment reliability for the next cycle.
- Precisely describe maintenance operations in Maximo.
- Use these procedures to structure consultations and purchase requests.
- Improve work tracking during shutdowns.
- Identify interventions with specific risks.
- Better control coactivity situations.
- Capitalize on lessons learned to improve future shutdowns.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Embed preparation in a multi-year vision

Shutdown preparation is often conducted with a short-term logic: identify work, select suppliers, and organize interventions before restart.

I advocated for a different approach.

I considered that preparation should also enable the long-term structuring of knowledge about equipment and maintenance operations.

This vision required significant upfront human investment but would provide a reference base for future campaigns.

### 2. Finance a year of in-depth preparation

I allocated part of my budgets to finance suppliers during **a year of preparation**.

This choice represented a significant commitment even before the start of shutdown work.

It was necessary to explain the approach, justify this investment, and maintain clear direction despite financial constraints and operational deadlines.

The objective was to convey that this effort benefited not only the immediate shutdown but also future interventions.

### 3. Describe maintenance procedures in Maximo

We undertook to describe maintenance procedures in detail within the CMMS **IBM Maximo**.

This work enabled structuring the operations to be performed and provided a common reference for all stakeholders.

The detailed breakdown of services constituted the core of the approach.

Rather than working from global descriptions, we sought to make interventions sufficiently explicit to facilitate their preparation, consultation, and follow-up.

### 4. Build consultations and purchases based on procedures

The documented procedures in Maximo were subsequently used to build purchase requests and service sheets.

This continuity between the technical reference and contractual documents allowed for better definition of expected works.

It also facilitated consultations and negotiations with service providers.

The precise breakdown of operations served as a lever for cost control by improving the readability of services to be performed.

### 5. Manage budgets and negotiate services

I managed the financial oversight of shutdowns, from preparation through to follow-up on execution.

The precision of technical descriptions contributed to better control over consultations, contracts, and services.

Savings achieved on these services enabled adherence to allocated budgets.

The objective was not simply to reduce costs, but to preserve a relevant balance between intervention costs and the reliability needs of installations.

### 6. Use procedures as support for site management

During shutdowns, procedures became a common tool for tracking works and operational meetings.

They enabled retrieval of planned interventions, tracking of execution, and facilitated exchanges between all involved parties.

This approach simplified site management by relying on a reference built during the preparation phase.

**The same framework thus accompanied works from their definition through to their execution.**

### 7. Strengthen risk and coactivity control

The detailed breakdown of operations also enabled better identification of services presenting particular risks.

This more precise knowledge of services facilitated anticipation of interfaces between intervenors and the organization of adapted preventive measures.

Safety was thus taken into account from the preparation stage, and not only during the execution of the project.

### 8. Capitalize lessons learned

The description of procedures should not become a static reference.

I designed the approach so that lessons from each shutdown could enrich existing descriptions.

Challenges encountered, necessary adaptations, and team feedback were to progressively improve the preparation of subsequent campaigns.

The objective was to have a **stable reference in its structure, but evolving in its content**.

## My Role

**As maintenance manager, I drove the vision, secured the necessary financial resources, and led the preparation and execution of shutdowns.**

The detailed construction of the reference framework and the execution of interventions relied on the collective work of teams and mobilized contractors.

## Technologies and Methods

**Tools:** Maximo (CMMS), maintenance procedures, purchase requests, service sheets, work tracking documents.

**Domains:** industrial maintenance, planned shutdowns, contract management, technical procurement, risk prevention, equipment reliability.

**Methods:** detailed preparation, structuring of interventions, planning, consultation and negotiation, budget monitoring, coordination of external companies, capitalization of lessons learned.

**Skills:** maintenance strategy, industrial project management, cross-functional leadership, cost control, risk management, continuous improvement.

## Results

### Budget Compliance

Shutdowns were executed within allocated budgets, notably thanks to savings achieved on services.

### Better Work Control

The detailed description of procedures enabled more precise tracking of interventions, from preparation to execution.

### Simplified Site Management

Using procedures as a common reference facilitated operational meetings and work monitoring.

### Enhanced Safety

Upstream identification of sensitive services and coactivity situations improved preparation and risk control.

### Reusable maintenance reference framework

The work carried out in Maximo provided a structured foundation for subsequent shutdowns, with the possibility of enriching it through lessons learned.

## Lessons Learned

### 1. Preparing for a shutdown is an investment, not merely an expense

Thorough preparation efforts can yield benefits far exceeding the immediate operation.

Value is built over multiple maintenance cycles.

### 2. Technical data accuracy improves financial control

Clearly described services facilitate consultations, negotiations, and tracking of completed work.

The quality of the reference library directly contributes to the quality of economic management.

### 3. A CMMS can become an operational management tool

Maximo was not used solely for recording maintenance operations.

By structuring work sequences, we turned it into a support for preparation, consultation, and tracking of tasks.

### 4. Safety is prepared from the definition of interventions

Detailed identification of work allows anticipating specific risks and coactivity situations.

Prevention benefits from being integrated into the preparation process rather than addressed belatedly.

### 5. The reliability of the next cycle is built during the previous shutdown

Decisions made regarding the content and quality of interventions condition the future operation of installations.

Budgetary control must therefore remain compatible with technical needs.

### 6. Lessons learned must enrich a lasting reference library

Each shutdown generates useful knowledge.

The challenge is to preserve this knowledge in a structured database so as not to have to start preparation from scratch for each campaign.

### 7. Management also consists of defending long-term investments

Funding one year of preparation required explaining the approach and maintaining course.

The difficulty was to preserve a sustainable vision in an environment subject to immediate budgetary constraints.

**My conviction: maintenance gains do not result from isolated actions. They are built over time, thanks to a coherent strategy, rigorous preparation, and systematic capitalization of knowledge.**

This project illustrates my ability to link strategic vision, maintenance data structuring, contractual control, budget management, and intervention safety to sustainably improve the preparation of industrial shutdowns.
