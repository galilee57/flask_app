# Lead the reorganization of an industrial workshop to streamline flows and reduce work-in-progress

*Lead an OPR (Operations Process Reengineering) initiative for workshop transformation, combining flow analysis, space reorganization, logistics optimization, and digitalization of operations.*

## Context

The RAC workshop, specialized in repair and finishing operations for industrial products, faced difficulties related to the accumulation of work-in-progress (WIP) and saturation of its storage spaces.

Products awaiting treatment occupied a significant surface area and complicated handling operations, equipment programming, and flow organization.

This situation resulted from several interdependent factors: incoming volumes, non-conformities, repair capacities, facility availability, logistics organization, and physical product management.

Reducing stock levels could therefore not rely solely on increasing equipment productivity.

**I led a cross-functional workshop reorganization initiative, leveraging an OPR approach to rethink flows, free up space, improve handling conditions, and modernize operational tools.**

## Objectives

- Reduce work-in-progress (WIP) and saturation situations.
- Streamline movements and handling operations.
- Recover available surfaces within the workshop.
- Optimize the organization of storage zones.
- Improve product visibility and accessibility.
- Reorganize scrap and residue management.
- Facilitate overhead crane operator work.
- Develop the use of digital tools in the field.
- Sustainably improve the overall performance of the workshop.

## Approach and implementation

### 1. Structure the OPR approach and mobilize teams

I coordinated an initiative to improve workshop organization by involving production teams, Supply Chain, logistics, quality, maintenance, and continuous improvement.

The objective was to build a shared diagnosis and define a coherent action plan, rather than addressing the difficulties encountered separately.

Management relied on regular meetings, prioritization of actions, and tracking their progress.

### 2. Analyze flows with Supply Chain

I worked with Supply Chain to analyze product flows entering the workshop, repair and finishing operations, waiting areas, and outputs.

This analysis allowed us to examine:

- Volumes and origins of work-in-progress (WIP).
- Available processing capacities.
- Scheduling constraints.
- Product movements.
- Overloaded storage areas.
- Access difficulties to products.
- Expectations related to quality decisions.
- Interactions between production and logistics.

One important point was distinguishing computer inventory from physically accessible inventory.

A product recorded as available could in practice be difficult to retrieve, poorly positioned, or blocked behind other products.

**The analysis allowed us to treat WIP as a global flow and organization problem, and not solely as a production capacity problem.**

### 3. Free up space by offloading to another workshop

To reduce saturation, I contributed to organizing the offloading of certain products to another workshop.

This action aimed to exploit complementary capacities and reduce volumes present in the RAC workshop.

Offloading allowed us to:

- Reduce pressure on storage areas.
- Free up surfaces for routine operations.
- Improve product accessibility.
- Facilitate material handling movements.
- More efficiently distribute loads between workshops.

This approach required coordination between production, scheduling, and Supply Chain teams.

### 4. Reorganize the scrap area and residue evacuation

I integrated the management of scrap and industrial residues into the workshop reorganization.

The objective was to free up unnecessarily occupied spaces and clarify areas designated for scrap products.

Actions focused on:

- Reorganization of the scrap area.
- Clarification of locations.
- Improvement of temporary storage conditions.
- Organization of residue evacuation.
- Limiting congestion in operational areas.

This reorganization contributed to improving surface utilization and circulation conditions.

### 5. Identify and mark locations for overhead crane operators

Product handling represented a central issue in the workshop's operation.

I worked on identifying locations to facilitate operations performed by overhead crane operators.

The objective was to make storage areas more readable and enable faster product location.

This organization aimed specifically to:

- Clearly identify available locations.
- Facilitate product positioning.
- Reduce searches and unnecessary movements.
- Improve coordination between programming and handling.
- Strengthen the reliability of physical stock location.

**Locating locations was a key lever to bring the workshop's physical organization closer to its computerized management.**

### 6. Engage digitalization of operations with 5G and tablets

The workshop transformation also included a digital component.

I integrated the use of 5G connectivity and industrial tablets into the approach to bring operational information closer to field teams.

The objective was to facilitate access to data and limit exchanges based on movements or disconnected supports from operations.

The sought-after uses included:

- Access to production information.
- Consultation of stock data.
- Identification and location of products.
- Transmission of information from the field.
- Improvement of coordination between teams.

This orientation aimed to support physical reorganization through modernization of work tools.

## My Role

**I led the workshop reorganization approach and coordinated cross-functional actions necessary for improving flows and reducing work-in-progress.**

My main contributions:

- Structuring of the OPR approach.
- Coordination of flow analyses with Supply Chain.
- Contribution to organizing product transfer to another workshop.
- Reorganization of storage areas and scrap zones.
- Consideration of residue evacuation.
- Improved location identification for overhead crane operators.
- Integration of a digitalization component based on 5G and tablets.

Material interventions, computer developments, and technical trade-offs were carried out collectively with the relevant business units.

## Results

- Reduction of work-in-progress
- Desaturation and reorganization of spaces
- Improved scrap management
- Better readability of storage areas
- Modernization of operational practices

## Lessons Learned

### Workshop performance depends on all its flows

Increasing production capacity is insufficient when stocks are hard to access, spaces are saturated, or logistics operations are poorly coordinated.

### Physical reorganization can generate quick gains

Transfer of load, scrap evacuation, and clarification of locations constitute concrete levers to regain operational capacity.

### Supply Chain must be integrated into industrial improvement

Flow organization depends equally on scheduling, product availability and logistics as on production equipment.

### Digitalization must support shop floor organization

Tablets and connectivity add value when they support clearly defined flows and reliable product location.

**This project demonstrates my ability to drive the transformation of an industrial workshop, combining flow improvement, space reorganization, team coordination and operations digitalization.**
